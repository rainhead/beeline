# The front page

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

`/` is one table (`src/app/dashboard.ts` + `src/app/views/qc.tsx`, reshaped with Peter and Nora on the sandbox, 2026-09-16): the person's samples that want something — a flag to fix, labels still to print — and the observations they numbered but left at 0 specimens, which minting refuses (`observation_sample_candidate`) and which are on the page for exactly that reason: a placeholder made in the field is what a volunteer forgets to fill in. Beside those are the observations in the project with their sample number left blank (`observation_unnumbered`, `schema/109`, beeline-a04): the three synced projects require `sampleId` to join and accept it empty, so `observation_field.sample_number_field_attached` keeps "attached but blank" apart from "absent", which `sample_number_raw` cannot. Their notes are quoted back, since some volunteers wrote the number there, and are never parsed for one: '1C red cuckoo' is why. Below the table, advisory, is the list of days whose run of sample numbers skips one (`sample_number_gap`, beeline-virz), each linked to everything the volunteer observed on iNaturalist that day, in the project or not, and wherever it was — the link says `place_id=any`, since iNaturalist otherwise narrows the list to the viewer's home place. Numbers restart every collecting day, so a gap is judged per day, and only for collectors who number that way. A jump of more than three starts a second run (`1, 2, 3, 101, 102`) rather than counting as missed samples. A day whose unnumbered observations account for its missing numbers is left out. A flag names the column it is about — sample, place, coordinates, host plant, specimens — and that cell is marked, with the flag's words on a full-width line under the row; `DASHBOARD_RULES` is the list of rules a volunteer sees here and the column each maps to, and a rule absent from it is not this page's business (a count that fell after printing means labels to discard, not a job; beeline-69t reviews the rest). The page never says "sync": it states the schedule — every morning at 2am Pacific — and not the last run's timestamp, which the sandbox printed in UTC as 9am and which is `/jobs`'s concern. Below it is **Check iNaturalist now** (`src/app/inat-check.ts`, beeline-yso), so a volunteer who has just fixed an observation sees the flag clear at once: it reads every observation the person made in the active seasons (this one, and last season until 1 March) from each synced project, one project at a time because trust is per project, whatever iNaturalist says changed (Peter mistrusts its `updated_at`, 2026-10-16), then runs the nightly's own promotion on `mintConn` behind the lock the unknown collectors' screen uses (`src/app/promotion-lock.ts`), with the app's own token, since volunteers' were dropped. Once a minute per observer, counted from the attempt; refused while the nightly or the Sunday sweep is reading iNaturalist, since both rewrite `observation_field` whole; acting for someone checks theirs, and impersonation sees the button and is refused. Each run records whose observations it read in `sync_run.observer_user_id`, which keeps it from moving the nightly's watermark (`lastSyncStart`) and lets deletion detection count it as covering that observer only. Above the table, a note says how many specimens nobody has named from the newest active season that has any — in January and February usually last season's, so the note names the year — linking to `/determinations` on that season; it counts what that screen calls "to name" (`entrySeasons`), against the signed-in determiner's drafts even while acting for someone, and is absent at zero. The specimen page points to the same screen when the viewer's own specimen has no volunteer determination. Settled seasons stay off it and are counted (beeline-2c3.24); a settled sample stays only while a run already holds its labels, until they are mailed, since a season's end does not cancel a print job already under way. The heading is the brand with a lede for a new volunteer, not a worklist title; the summary — "2 samples need attention" — is the second heading, since it is what the volunteer came for, and the schedule note sits under the table. Where the person belongs to an atlas (`person_membership`, never where their samples fell), the header ends in that atlas's mark, as it does on their `/people` page; the marks and colorways live in one table, `ATLAS_IDENTITY` (`src/app/theme/atlas.ts`), and are for now the 216px JPEGs the program distributes on Canvas, cropped to their circle and drawn small until the vector artwork arrives (beeline-2c3.13).

The host cell draws a host identified to a taxon Beeline has no name for as that iNaturalist taxon, linked, rather than as absent (gh-250): the projection occasionally holds an id and no name, and "none" beside a flag saying the host is not a plant read as a contradiction. An observation with no identification, for a bee that was not on a plant, is never flagged, and the flag's instruction says so.

A staff hold (beeline-plu) is one more flag on the page, `staff_hold`, with no column of its own: its note says what is wrong. A flag staff have waived (beeline-1kb.9) is not on the page at all, because the page reads `sample_qc_finding`, which leaves it out while it says what it said when waived.

A locality flag — `locality_format`, or a required field missing that is the locality — carries on its line the locality Beeline names from the sample's coordinates, with a button that makes it the sample's locality in one click, rather than the volunteer guessing at a name on iNaturalist ([Localities from coordinates](localities-from-coordinates.md), beeline-7i6).

Last on the page, below everything that wants doing, is **Latest identifications of your bees** (`latestIdentifications` in `src/app/dashboard.ts`, beeline-xegx): the ten newest expert identifications of the person's specimens, a second collector's included, each with the name an expert gave before where it changed one. It reads `expert_identification` (`schema/111`), which turns a specimen's expert determinations into a story: consecutive determinations with the same name and qualifier are one identification, because a new determination is not always news — Ecdysis's loader records the identification it calls current as a new event even when it repeats what the old Beeline held, and its first night on production recorded 36,359 of them, which this rule cut to the 14,551 that named a bee or changed a name. It is ordered by when the bee was identified, not when Beeline heard: a day the determiner stated, else the moment it was entered in Ecdysis, else when an expert's sheet was sent — which is only the latest the bee could have been identified, and the page says "on or before" that day, since none of the Idaho sheet's 1,832 determinations states a date — else when Beeline recorded one made in Beeline. Those identifications were entered between February and November 2026, and dated by the load they would all have read as one night. The old Beeline's expert determinations carry no date, so a name only it ever held is left off rather than guessed at. Volunteers' own determinations are not listed: they are not news to the person who made them. The query first narrows to the person's specimens with an expert determination something can date, and passes them to the view as a list of ids, which DuckDB pushes below the view's window functions; production's most prolific collector has 40,326 specimens and 746 qualify, and the page costs about 3% more than without the feed on a copy of production's store. Telling a volunteer what is new since they last looked, or by email, waits for a stored marker of when they last did.

First in the header bar, beside Samples and Specimens (Andony, gh-262), is **How Beeline works** (`/how-it-works`, `src/app/views/how-it-works.tsx`, beeline-kgwd): the process in the specimen's order, from the observation on iNaturalist to handing the season's specimens in, with what each step needs from the volunteer and when things happen. The front page's lede links to it. Like the glossary it is catalog copy throughout, and a test checks that every link on it resolves, so its links cannot go stale; the hours and dates it states are written by hand and change with the schedule in `src/app/jobs/registry.ts`.
