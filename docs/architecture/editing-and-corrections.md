# Editing and corrections

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

Non-iNat samples are edited in-app (`/samples/:id/edit`, collector-gated): each save updates the live sample row and records correction events keyed by staging Mongo `_id` in `data/corrections.csv` (`src/corrections.ts` — outside the blow-away path), which legacy promotion reads union the git-curated `ingest/legacy-corrections.csv`, app rows winning ([ADR 0004](../adr/0004-correction-overlay.md), beeline-2c3.8). Staff set a sample's **locality** from its page (`/samples/:id`, admin-gated, refused while impersonating; `src/app/locality-override.ts`, beeline-649), because an unprinted iNat-linked sample's locality follows its observation on every promotion and nobody could edit one in the app, so a place name the observation cannot yield had nowhere to go. One form, two durable stores, since the two kinds of sample are named by different things a rebuild reproduces: a sample with an observation goes to `data/sample-overlay.csv` (`src/sample-overlay.ts`, the person overlay's shape plus a `base_value`, keyed `inat:<observation id>` — the link is the sample's identity, and `entity_id` is a per-store draw), applied as the satellite `sample_locality_override` (schema/030) by `src/apply-sample-overlay.ts` at the end of observation promotion and at once from the page; a sample with none goes through the corrections overlay as the collector's own edit would. The follow rule in `ingest/mint-samples.sql` skips overridden samples, `sample_locality_override_stale` asserts the row and the sample agree, and `sample_locality_override_diverged` (schema/175) names an observation that has since moved to a third value, which the page shows beside the override rather than resolving: a deliberate assertion beats an unreviewed upstream edit (ADR 0004). Removing the override hands an unprinted sample back to its observation at once; a printed one keeps what the label says. Promotion reads the file only when given its path, so a scratch promotion never reads the deployed store's decisions; the nightly reports rows that name no sample in the run's detail and goes on. The coordinates take the same form (beeline-942): a second field in the same file, `coordinates`, valued `<latitude> <longitude> [uncertainty m]`, applied as `sample_location_override` with `sample_location` written beside it as `staff_entry`, the provenance every read already carries — and the location upgrade in `ingest/promote-observations.sql`, which used to rewrite `sample_location` unconditionally and so erased a staff point on the next nightly, skips overridden samples. The base it records is the `sample_location` row as it stood, whatever its source, so removal hands an unprinted sample back exactly what it had, an imported point included, and a sample that had none goes back to none; the elevation is left for `sample_elevation_stale` to notice, never cleared by the writer. The observation's believed-true point is now the view `observation_location` (schema/105), which promotion's candidate table reads and the diverged view compares against, with `sample_elevation_stale`'s 5e-5° tolerance. The form appears on the coordinates block even where the sample has none, because that is the case it exists for: an obscured observation with no trusted access yields no coordinates and cannot print, and only a person can put the true point in. Offered only on samples with an observation: the corrections overlay would land an imported sample's point as `legacy_import`, misstating who said so. A staff point carries the county, state and country of where it lies, printed or not ([Areas](areas.md)).

**Where the rules for a correction live** (beeline-pnqr.8). The routes for the collector's edit, `/suggested-locality`, `/place`, `/coordinates`, `/take-observation-location`, `/hold` and `/waiver` only read the form, call one function in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts` and turn its result into a response. The function (`editSample`, `adoptLocality`, `setPlace`, `setCoordinates`, `takeLocation`, `holdSample`, `waiveFlag`, and `setTrapCount`, `setSpecimenCount`, `setSamplePlace` and `setCollectors`, below) takes who is acting (an `Actor`, made by `actorFor`, which never yields staff while impersonating), the sample and the input, and holds every rule: refusing while impersonating, the staff gate, a locality somebody else set standing, a printed sample, only an offered name unless staff, answering not found before printed when the sample was entered in Beeline and the person may not edit it (so a stranger is not told it exists), refusing while a sync job runs, deriving elevation and county after a point moves (`deriveMovedPoint`), and recording the change under its author. The input is a value or a function that reads it; a page passes a function that parses the form, and the correction calls it only after the rules that do not depend on the form have passed, and before it takes its turn of the per-sample queue (next paragraph), so a refused request's body is never read. It returns `{ ok: true, ... }` or `{ ok: false, refusal }` with a named refusal, which the route turns into the message and status it has always answered with (each route's switch ends in `unreachable`, so a refusal left unhandled fails the typecheck), so the API calls the same function and maps the refusals to its own errors ([the API](the-api-and-its-keys.md), beeline-pnqr.6). The API passes the input as a value, and adds two things the pages do not use: an `outcome` on every success (`saved`, `removed` or `unchanged`, as the writer reported it) and, for the collector's edit, a `dryRun` option that plans the edit in the same queue turn and writes nothing. The actor's `via` says which door the request came through, and is recorded as the history entry's source (`app` for a page, `api` for the API). The two reasons a correction writes for itself, for an adopted locality and for taking the observation's location, are built in `src/app/correction-reasons.ts` from the message catalogue, so a page and the API store the same words. Every one of them runs one at a time for its sample (next paragraph). `/method` still has its rules in the route, and takes its own turn of the same queue. The collector's edit is split into `planSampleEdit` (the events and column values, no writes) and `writeSampleEdit` (the overlay file, then the store), which `applySampleEdit` composes; a dry run needs the first alone. `writeSampleEdit` is not exported, because a plan is only valid inside the queue turn that made it: writing an older plan after a newer save would overwrite that save. Still no retry on a write conflict, for the reason in [ingestion jobs](ingestion-jobs-and-elevation.md). `test/sample-corrections.test.ts` pins the refusals and the saves without a page, and `test/sample-corrections-routes.test.ts` the author when acting for someone, impersonation through the pages, and that a refused request with an unreadable form still gets its refusal.

**One correction at a time for a sample** (beeline-4ksk). Every one of those functions runs in the same shape (`correct` in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts`). The rules that do not depend on the form are asked first, so a request that will be refused is refused without its form being read. The form is then read, outside the sample's queue, so a slow upload holds up nobody else's save. Then one turn of the per-sample queue (`src/app/sample-queue.ts`) asks those rules again, of the sample as it stands now, checks what depends on the form, writes, derives what follows from the write, and records the change in the sample's history. The second asking is the one the write relies on. Because the whole of that is one turn, two corrections to one sample cannot interleave: neither writes between the other's check and its write, the localities a collector may adopt are the ones offered in that turn and not when the request arrived, and each history entry is taken before the next correction begins, under its own author. Until this, only the collector's edit and the place editor took a turn. The other five did not, and the history is recorded from the sample's state after the fact (ADR 0007), so a collector adopting a locality while staff held the same sample could have both changes entered under one of their names, and a collector could replace a locality staff had saved a moment earlier. A turn is taken once the input is in hand. A caller that already holds it, as the API does, skips the first asking and takes its turn in the same tick it was called, so corrections called one after another are applied in the order they were called. That is the order of calls, not of requests: over HTTP, two requests sent together may reach the call in either order (each looks up its key, or reads its form, first), so a program that needs an order waits for the first answer ([the API](the-api-and-its-keys.md)). A staff point is entered in the history straight after it is written and again after its elevation and county are derived: deriving waits for "Check iNaturalist now" if one is running, and that check ends with a pass over every sample's history under no author, which would otherwise take the staff point for its own. (A check that ends in the instant between the write and that first entry can still take it: the history is read from state after the fact, so that window can be made small and not closed.) The edit form of a sample entered in Beeline takes a turn of the same queue for its write and history entry. Nothing a turn calls may take a turn for the same sample itself, since the queue does not nest.

**How a sample from the old Beeline was collected** (beeline-p8y1). Staff correct a sample's method and the day collecting ended from a pencil on its Method row (`POST /samples/:id/method`, `src/app/sample-method.ts`, admin-gated, refused while impersonating), offered on any sample built from the old Beeline's records, with an iNaturalist observation or without. The collector's form refuses a sample with an observation because a correction has to name an old record and an observation-only sample has none; a sample built from old records has them whether or not it was linked, and the nightly sync never rewrites a linked sample's method or dates, so the correction stays. A save is the collector's edit in shape: one event per old record and field in `data/corrections.csv` (`samplingProtocol`, and `day2`/`month2`/`year2` for the end), written before the live sample changes, then a pass of the sample change log credited to whoever saved. The kind is not a field of its own: promotion makes a sample a trap sample exactly when its records give an end date, so an end date given here makes it one and none makes it a net sample collected on its start day, what a rebuild would make of the corrected records. **The start date is offered where iNaturalist does not hold it**, on a sample with no observation (Peter, 2026-11-15, beeline-0wbq): one event per old record for `day`, `month` and `year`, and a net sample's end moves with it. It was left out at first because promotion groups records into samples by their start date; a corrected start moves every record of the sample together, so a development store rebuilt from the corrections makes the same sample, or merges it into another of the collector's with that number and day, which is then what the records say, and production is never promoted again. **A trap sample entered in Beeline** is edited on its own form until its labels print or are frozen into a run ([Entering trap samples](entering-trap-samples.md)); after that this form opens for it too, changes its method and dates on the sample directly, since it has no old records to name, and keeps it a trap sample whatever the end date. A changed date reaches a prepared run's labels as out of date (`printed_label_out_of_date`), and a printed sample is reprinted by staff as any corrected record. The change log follows a sample whose start moved by its id, which it believes only on a store that was built (`idsBelong`, `src/sample-change.ts`): production is, and a test store has to say so. A save from either form also writes its events into the store's own correction tables (`src/app/correction-store.ts`), which promotion builds from the files and `legacy_occurrence_corrected` reads the old records through: production is never promoted again, and the legacy-format export and the copy into old Beeline write each record's own method and end date where a sample's records disagree, so without it a correction reached the page and never the export (the independent review of #314). If the store write loses a write conflict, the page answers 409 with the collector's edit's sentence (`sampleEdit.notApplied`) rather than the failure page: for a sample with old records the events are already in the file, which nothing applies later on production, and for an entered sample nothing was kept, so either way the change is shown only once it is saved again, which does not record it twice (beeline-kgyd.4.8). Saves to one sample run one at a time across the collector's form, the staff place editor and this one, each with its entry in the change log (`src/app/sample-queue.ts`). Two effects matter only on a recent sample, not on the imported ones this was made for: widening a sample's dates widens the range `sample_mint_pending` refuses to mint a later observation of the same collector and number into, as any trap sample's does, and a label in a prepared, unprinted run is not marked out of date by a change of method alone. It was made for Pat Wheeler's six trap samples of 2021, which Beeline published as netted on one day because the copy promotion kept was the one the old Beeline printed from the observation, with its default "aerial net" ([the legacy-format export](the-legacy-format-export.md)).

**A trap sample's trap count** (beeline-kgyd.4.1). The count is not a column: it is the leading number of `sample.sampling_effort` ("6 vane traps"), written by `trapEffort` and read by `trapCountOf` in `src/trap-sample.ts`, which is also how `setSampleMethod` keeps it in step when the method changes. Promotion of the old Beeline's samples never writes it, so those have none. Nothing prints or exports it (not the labels, the legacy-format export or Darwin Core); the sample record, the API and the history show it. `setTrapCount` (`src/app/sample-corrections.ts`, `POST /api/v1/samples/:id/trap-count`) lets staff set it on any trap sample, printed or not, since nothing prints it: a positive whole number writes `trapEffort(count, method)` with the sample's current method, and a blank takes it away. A net sample, and a sample with no method, are refused with a sentence saying what to do (a count is kept beside the kind of trap). The same count is `unchanged`. It runs in the sample's queue and is recorded in the history under the staff member, as `sampling_effort`. There is no overlay file: the count has no old record to be kept against, and production is never promoted again (ADR 0006), so a development store rebuilt from the old records loses it. A method text that happens to contain a count ("6 pan traps" in an old sample's `protocol`) is left alone. On the sample page staff get a second pencil on the Method row of any trap sample (`TrapCountEdit` in `src/app/views/record.tsx`, `POST /samples/:id/trap-count`), separate from the method's own pencil so that `setSampleMethod` is unchanged and the count can be corrected on a sample whose method form is closed. Its refusals are catalogue sentences (`record.sample.trapCount.problems`), handed to the function as `problemFor` so the page and the API say the same words. `test/trap-count-page.test.ts` pins the pencil and the route. The samples upload (beeline-kgyd.4) is the other caller.

**A trap sample's specimen count** (beeline-kgyd.4.4). Staff correct `sample.specimen_count` of a trap sample, printed or not, through the API only (`setSpecimenCount` in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts`, `POST /samples/:id/specimen-count`); there is no pencil or page for it. Raising it makes the next print run number the extra bees, because the freeze creates specimens for the places in 1..count that no individuated specimen holds (`pending_print_sample`, schema/150). Lowering it below the highest field number already given to the sample is refused, but lowering to exactly that number is allowed. A sample whose count still follows its observation is refused: any sample with an observation and no label on paper, since `ingest/mint-samples.sql` rewrites the count of such a sample at each promotion. That is wider than the promotion's own condition, which skips an observation whose count is zero or not a number, because the volunteer fixing that observation would undo the staff count without a word. The volunteer changes the count on iNaturalist instead. On a printed sample with an observation the change is allowed and the `count_mismatch` flag stands, which is expected. The check and the write run behind the print runs' lock (`withPrintRunLock`), so a freeze cannot slip between them. The answer carries `pending`, how many labels the next run would print; it is 0 for a sample from a settled season (schema/102), which never prints, so raising a 2019 sample's count prints nothing (open as beeline-3vs.7). Like the trap count, it is a direct write recorded in the sample's history with no overlay file, because production is never promoted again (ADR 0006); a development store rebuilt from the old records loses it.

**A printed sample whose pin moved** (beeline-1kb.17.1, GitHub #298). Once labels print, the point stops following iNaturalist, so a collector who moves the pin afterwards leaves the record agreeing with a label that may be wrong. `sample_location_label_diverged` (schema/175) names a sample Beeline printed a label for, with no staff point, whose observation now lies more than 100 m from it (the label's three decimals, the distance beeline-1kb.17 settled on). On the sample page staff see where iNaturalist puts it now and one button, "Use iNaturalist's location" (`POST /samples/:id/take-observation-location`, `takeObservationLocation`), which is the staff coordinates write with the observation's point as its value, read from the store rather than the form. The labels on paper are untouched and a reprint is asked for as any other. Samples whose labels the old Beeline printed are left out: on the store of 2026-11-10 the same test found 542 of them, all imported points a median 3 km from their observations and some an ocean away, which say the old system held a different point or linked the wrong observation rather than that someone moved a pin. Showing those, and the volunteer's side, is still beeline-1kb.17. The country, state and county come from `observation_place` only for the line on the page, in a second query made when the view finds something, because joined into the view it cost every printed sample's page 140 ms.

**A staff point on a sample with no observation** (beeline-kgyd.4.2). Staff correct the point of a sample built from the old Beeline's records, or entered in Beeline, from the same pencil on the Coordinates row (`setCoordinates`, `setStaffCoordinates` in `src/app/locality-override.ts`). It is the observation sample's staff point, kept in the sample overlay under a second kind of key: `legacy:<_id>`, an old record of the sample, resolved through `legacy_specimen_number` (`resolveOldRecordSample` in `src/apply-sample-overlay.ts`). A later save reuses the `legacy:` key already standing in the file for the sample, even after an observation has been linked to it (and a removal then replaces that row), and failing that takes the sample's first (lowest) record, so a sample never has two rows; the applier applies a removal under such a key only for a sample that has an observation now. Not corrections events on those records like the method's (`setSampleMethod`), because an event's point comes back from a rebuild as `legacy_import` with no override row to carry the source, county, state, atlas and elevation with it, and the legacy-format export writes the sample's own point only once its source is `staff_entry`; and not the collector, number and date, which staff can all correct. A store with no old records reports the row unresolved, as for an observation it has not seen, and the nightly goes on. A sample entered in Beeline is named by nothing a file could hold, so its override row is written straight to the store (`applyStaffPoint`); a form edit of such a sample (`updateTrapSample`) rewrites the point and drops the override. The county, state and country follow the point as for any staff point (`src/derive-area.ts` follows a sample with an override whatever its kind). **There is no removal for these samples, deliberately** (Peter, 2026-11-18): there is no observation to follow again, and putting back what an imported or entered sample held (its point, place, atlas and elevation) took three review rounds to find it needs more of the past than the store keeps, since promotion took each field from whichever record had one and later repairs added records. Staff correct a wrong point by setting the right one. The page offers no remove button, `setCoordinates` refuses a removal with its reason, and a removal row naming a `legacy:` key in the file is reported unresolved, never applied and never thrown. For a sample with an observation removal is as it was (the held point back, unless labels have printed). A point change, a removal included, is refused on any sample while a print run that has not printed holds its labels (`sample_label_in_progress`, the check the trap form and the county stamp make), because the run froze the point it carries. Saving the point deletes a stored finding that the old records disagreed about it (the save only: a replay of the file does not, so the nightly does not delete it again). The write is one transaction on the print connection behind the print runs' lock (`withPrintRunLock`, as the trap form's are: every writer on that connection takes the lock, so nothing else is inside a transaction there), never on the app's shared connection, where another request's statements would join it and a rollback would undo them; the waivers are refreshed after the lock is released, and `pnpm sample:apply` opens the store alone. A staff point on an entered sample writes no file row, so a write conflict there is answered `busy` (nothing was done) and not "recorded". An instance with no separate print connection falls back to the shared one under the lock. No migration: the point's merge base (`observed_*`) was already recorded. The legacy-format export writes the sample's country, state and county rather than each old record's own wherever an override stands, and `legacy:compare-export` calls the resulting differences `staff_point`.

**A sample's country, state and county** (beeline-kgyd.4.3). Staff correct them through the API (`POST /api/v1/samples/:id/country-state-county`) and, next, the samples upload (beeline-kgyd.4); no page offers it yet (Peter, 2026-11-18). `setSamplePlace` in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts` holds the gates (staff only, refused while impersonating) and `setStaffPlace` in `src/app/sample-place.ts` the rest, all behind the print runs' lock. A sample built from the old Beeline's records, with an observation or without, is corrected as the collector's own edit corrects one (`applySampleEdit`): one event per record and field in `data/corrections.csv` (`country`, `stateProvince`, `county`), mirrored into the store's correction tables, then the sample, and a disagreement among the records over a changed field is settled. So the legacy-format export writes the corrected place on every record, `legacy:compare-export` calls the difference `staff_correction`, and a development store rebuilt from the file makes the same sample. A country goes into the records as the old Beeline wrote it (CA, NZ and MX for CAN, NZL and MEX, `legacyCountry`), which the collector's edit now does too, and promotion reads MX back as MEX, as it already read CA and NZ. Any other sample, entered in Beeline or minted from an observation, has no record outside the store, so the sample is written directly. Printed samples are corrected like any other, since the correction comes before a reprint. A changed state or country moves the atlas where geography assigned it, in the same transaction, as the nightly's stamp does ([Areas](areas.md)); an atlas a person assigned stays. The region is matched on the country as well as the state, where `sample_atlas_unfilled` matches on the state alone, so a sample corrected to Western Australia is given no atlas by its WA. The answer lists what changed, the atlas included. **Refused where the place follows the point**: on a sample with a staff point, or one still to print that has an observation or was entered in Beeline, the nightly writes the county, state and country from the point (`followsPointSql` in `src/derive-area.ts`, the one definition the stamp and this share), so a correction would be gone by morning and staff correct the point instead. Such a sample is let through only where the areas step found its present point in no county or in two, under the current rule and the newest gazetteer any sample was tested against (a point tested against an older one is tested again that night), since the nightly then writes nothing; on production's store of 18 November 2026 the samples that follow their point are the 107 net samples from this season still to print and the two printed ones with staff points. **Refused while a print run that has not printed holds the sample**, as a point is, since the run froze the place its labels carry. **A blank county is refused on a sample with an observation**, because minting fills an empty county from iNaturalist on every promotion. Refused as `busy` while the nightly or the weekly sweep runs, asked again in the sample's queue turn. A value that would print wrong is refused with a sentence: a country that is not three letters; a state that is not two or three, or, for the USA or Canada, whose lists in `atlas_region` are complete, one not listed under that country (`BC is in CAN, not USA.`); and a county with a comma or, in the USA, ending in "County", since the label adds "Co". Any other country takes any state code of two or three letters, since a code is unique only within its country (WA is also Western Australia). Recorded in the history under the staff member. A write conflict on an old sample comes after the corrections file holds the events, and production never reads that file again (ADR 0006), so the API says the change is recorded and applied only by sending it again (`not_applied`); on any other sample nothing was recorded, and the answer is `busy`. `test/sample-place.test.ts` pins it, the rebuild, the export and the comparison included.

**A sample's collectors** (beeline-kgyd.4.5). Staff add, remove and reorder who collected a sample, the first included, through the API (`POST /api/v1/samples/:id/collectors`) and, next, the samples upload (beeline-kgyd.4); no page offers it yet (Peter, 2026-11-18). `setCollectors` in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts` holds the gates (staff only, refused while impersonating, `busy` while the nightly or the weekly sweep runs) and `setStaffCollectors` in `src/app/sample-collectors.ts` the rest. The list is given as entries in order, matched by `src/collector-match.ts`, the rule the trap form uses too: a person's id, or `inat:` and their login or user id; else a display name exactly; else the name folded to its letters and digits without case or accents. A name two people answer to is refused, as is one with no letters, one person named twice and an empty list, and nobody is created; a collector at the trap form is told to ask staff, since the pages that add and tell people apart are staff's. That rule reads display names alone, so it differs from the person overlay's resolver, which also knows every spelling promotion recorded ([Collectors Beeline doesn't know](collectors-beeline-doesnt-know.md)), and from promotion's `legacy_name_key`, which drops a letter outside a to z rather than reading it as its base letter; on production's store of 18 November 2026 no display name has such a letter and no two fold together. A list that reads exactly as the sample's does now is `unchanged` before any name is matched, so repeating a sample's collectors is never refused over a name. **On a sample built from the old Beeline's records** each record gets one `recordedBy` event in `data/corrections.csv`, the display names joined with " | " as the export writes them, mirrored into the store's correction tables; `recordedBy` is a correctable field of the old records since this change, and migration 0109 recreated the three views that read the corrections on production, which is never promoted again (ADR 0006). The name columns are never corrected: promotion groups records into samples by `firstName` and `lastName`, and so do carry-over, the held-back page and the repair of what promotion set aside, which still read the old records on production, so a corrected name could have carry-over mint a second sample under the old collector (12 held-back records on one trap sample on that store would have been exposed). So a development store rebuilt from a correction that changed the first collector can put the old one back first, with the new one beside them. A sample entered in Beeline or made from an observation changes `sample_collector` alone. **What the first collector carries**: the sample's number is theirs for the day, its labels are mailed to them (`src/app/print-run-addresses.ts`), and minting, the claims of observations and account harvesting find the sample through their iNaturalist account. So a new first collector is refused (`number_taken`) where they already have a sample with this number on this start date, and (`observation_tied`) where the sample cites an observation, one claims it (`sample_claiming_observation`), or an unlinked observation of the old or new first collector carries its number on its dates, because a trap's second observation, which minting deliberately passed over, could then be minted as a new sample and its pins labelled twice. Changing the first collector of such a sample waits for minting to key on the observer (beeline-kgyd.4.7); collectors after the first may still change on it. Who may see a sample's location where iNaturalist hides it, and who may edit it, follow every collector, so the answer warns of anyone added or removed as well as of a new first collector. **Refused while a print run that has not printed holds the sample**, since the run froze its collector line. The labels on paper print the old list, so the answer gives `reprint_needed`, how many there are, and staff ask for the reprint; nothing is requested here. The reads that decide the answer, matching and the observation checks among them, run first; then, behind the print runs' lock, the print run, the list and the sample's number, dates and cited observation (`conflict` if any changed meanwhile, as a trap form save or the nightly's free link could) and, for a new first collector, their number and whether an observation is tied are asked again, the events go to the file, and one transaction on the print connection replaces the sample's `sample_collector` rows, mirrors the events, and rolls back unless `sample_primary_collector_invalid` (schema/116) is empty for the sample. A lost write conflict on an old sample is `not_applied`, the file holding the events, and `busy` on any other. `dry_run` answers `would_save` with the same list, warnings and count and writes nothing. Recorded in the history under the staff member: the change log follows a sample whose first collector moved by its id, as for a moved start date. `test/sample-collectors.test.ts` pins it, with the export and the comparison, and `test/sample-collectors-migration.test.ts` the migration.

**The method and dates are a correction like the rest** (beeline-kgyd.4.11). `setMethod` in `src/app/sample-corrections.ts` takes who is acting (an `Actor`), the sample and the input as a value or a function, and is called by the method page's route (`POST /samples/:id/method`, whose responses and sentences are unchanged) and by the samples upload. It holds what `setSampleMethod` (`src/app/sample-method.ts`, the rules and the writes) does not: the sample's own turn of the queue with the history entry before the turn ends, the print runs' lock, a refusal (`in_print_run`) while a print run that has not printed holds the sample, and a refusal (`number_taken`) when a net sample given an end day would become a trap sample whose number another trap sample has. Its refusals are `invalid` (with the sentence the caller's `problemFor` gives), `no_old_records`, `conflict` (a touched field changed since the `bases` the caller saw), `not_applied` (the write lost a conflict, with the old records' events already in the corrections file), and the usual ones. `keepTrap` reads a blank end on a trap sample as one day, so it stays a trap sample.

**The samples upload is a caller of each correction** (beeline-kgyd.4.11). Applying a changed row of a staff member's samples file calls `setMethod`, `setTrapCount`, `setSpecimenCount`, `setCollectors`, `setCoordinates`, `setSamplePlace` and `setPlace` for the sample, one kind of change at a time, with an `Actor` whose `via` is `upload` (history source `upload`, author the staff member, reason "From upload N", the same note passed as each correction's note; `setPlace` takes an optional `note` for this, which the pages and the API leave out, and an adopted suggestion keeps it beside its own reason). The corrections are called with their input as a value and from outside any turn of the sample's queue, so each takes its own turn. A correction's refusal is recorded beside the change in the upload's outcome table in a sentence for a person (`refusalFor` in `src/app/samples-upload-change.ts`, the API's own where it says nothing untrue); `busy` and `not_applied` are not refusals, and leave the change pending. The rules, the order and what the preview predicts are in [Loading an edited download](loading-an-edited-download.md#applying-the-changes-to-existing-samples-beeline-kgyd411).

Staff **holds** and **waivers** (beeline-plu, beeline-1kb.9; *Hold* and *Waiver* in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md)) are two more fields in the same file, set from the flags card on `/samples/:id` (`src/app/qc-decisions.ts`, admin-gated, refused while impersonating) and applied by the same applier: `hold`, valued `held`, becomes a `sample_hold` row, and `waiver:<rule>`, valued `waived`, becomes a `sample_qc_waiver` row whose `base_value` is what the flag said when it was waived — the thing the waiver is tied to, as a locality override's base is what the observation said (schema/050). A reason is required for both, and the applier refuses a standing one without, rather than inserting a row the CHECK would fail the whole promotion over; it refuses a waiver on an unknown, non-blocking or unwaivable flag the same way, and `sample_qc_finding` ignores one on an unwaivable flag even if it reaches the table. A hold is a finding (`qc_rule_staff_hold`), so printability, the front page and the listings need nothing of their own; a waiver takes its finding out of `sample_qc_finding` while it is in force — the flag still says what it said when waived — which the applier settles each time it runs (`refreshWaivers`, after every promotion and every staff decision) and stores as `in_force`, because comparing the text in the view made every finding build its text and printability 80% slower with no waivers at all. `observation_missing_upstream` says when the observation was last returned rather than counting the runs that missed it, so a waiver on it does not lapse at the next sync. Waivers are also rechecked at the end of every sync and every person-overlay application, since a completed run and a moved iNaturalist account both change what a flag says before any promotion; and `qc_rule_sample_number` joins its two cases into one finding per sample, because a waiver names a flag by its sample and rule. Only samples with an observation take either, since the overlay names a sample by it and every sample still waiting to print has one.
