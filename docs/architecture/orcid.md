# ORCID

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#architecture-overview).

An ORCID iD credits a person in the records Beeline publishes: the specimens CSV, the samples CSV and the program archives write it as `https://orcid.org/<iD>` in Darwin Core's `recordedByID` (each collector who has one, in `recordedBy` order and separated the same way) and `identifiedByID` (the determiner, on the occurrence and on every row of the Identification extension), beeline-0544. Two sources, and **the person's own wins** (Peter, 2026-10-10): the iD their iNaturalist account has connected, which they proved by signing in to ORCID, else the one staff recorded — `person_orcid_of_record` (`schema/103`) is that rule, and `person_orcid_shared` names an iD that would credit two people, as a household's shared login connected to the partner's would. What iNaturalist says is `inat_user_orcid`, keyed by the iNat user id and written **at sign-in** from `/users/me` rather than on a schedule, since somebody told to connect one is somebody who signs in; `pnpm inat:fetch-orcids` asks for every bound account at once, for people who never do (2 of 440 had connected one on 2026-10-10). What staff record is `person_orcid`, through the overlay's `orcid` field on `/people/:id`, which refuses an iD that fails its check digit or that somebody else already holds; `pnpm person:orcids <file>` records a list of confirmed iDs (`display_name,orcid,confirmed_by,confirmed_on,how`) the same way, all or nothing. That list stays outside git: a volunteer's ORCID beside their name is personal data. The person change log keeps the two apart (`orcid`, `inat_orcid`), because which one credits somebody is a rule and not something that happened to them. A determiner known only by name gets none. Whether any of it goes out is each program's privacy policy (beeline-rp33).
