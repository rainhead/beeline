# And to a sample

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

Samples get the same treatment (beeline-ewl, the second instance of [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-authored-changes-are-events.md)): `data/sample-change.csv` (`src/sample-change.ts`) is append-only events, produced by the same compare-against-what-was-last-said pass at the end of legacy promotion, after observation promotion, and at boot — so the nightly's coordinate rewrites, minting, free-linking, and a staff edit all leave a trace, and a writer that forgets is caught by the next pass. The edit screen runs a pass narrowed to its one sample, so the author is credited with their edit and nothing else. Where it deliberately differs from the person log: the baseline lives in `data/sample-state.csv` — one current row per sample, restated wholesale each pass — because the person log's in-log baseline over this corpus would be a million rows; the reference is the derived triple (collector as the overlay names them, number, start date), with the observation link recognising a sample across a corrected number or date — and outranking the triple where a sample carries a different observation from the one recorded and another sample now carries that one, which is what splitting two observations that shared a number leaves (a renumbered sample, and a newcomer on its old number) — and a triple collision (a live duplicate) recorded as nothing and counted. Two gaps in that matching were found by property testing before cutover (2026-10-30): the number-and-date tier now sees the samples on a shared triple, whose links and collectors are still the store's even though the pass cannot speak for them, so a stranger with the same number and date no longer inherits a colliding sample's row; and the edit screen's one-sample pass asks the store how many samples derive its triple (`tripleCountSql`), because it holds one sample and could not see that a renumbering had made it a duplicate, and then filed the edit as changes to the other sample's history. The record page reads it as the History panel on `/samples/:id`, from a parse the app takes at boot and extends with its own appends, since the log is 15 MB on the sandbox and re-parsing it after every nightly cost the day's first sample page about 1.5 s. Measured on the dev store: baseline 1.1 s and 11.2 MB, a steady pass ~2 s and zero entries, and a nightly-shaped 30 moves + 2 renumbers recording exactly 32. The snapshot is a statement about one store, and a full pass **refuses** one that mostly names samples the store does not hold — no entries, no restatement, and a message naming the file — because the vanishing rule records silence and a store that has never seen a sample records an arrival, so a scratch store pointed at the shared `data/` paths turns one boot into a million-row append on the next real pass (the screenshot fixture did exactly that on 2026-09-21, and the 1,085,665 rows appeared on 2026-09-23; beeline-hrw). Refusing rather than re-baselining is what makes the mistake cost nothing in either direction: the fixture's own boot leaves the real snapshot alone. A store that has genuinely replaced the snapshot's re-baselines by deleting the file, and a snapshot with no rows is read as no snapshot. On the nightly a refusal **fails the run**, after the elevation step so nothing the store needs waits on it, because a refusal is a standing condition that needs a person and a green `/healthz/jobs` over an unrecorded history would hide it; the CLIs and the boot pass warn. Start a scratch app with `BEELINE_SAMPLE_CHANGES`/`BEELINE_SAMPLE_STATE`/`BEELINE_PERSON_CHANGES` pointed away from `data/` regardless; the person log has no snapshot to protect and simply gains the fixture's people as arrivals.

**The snapshot carries each sample's id** (beeline-7r0g, [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-authored-changes-are-events.md)'s addendum of 2026-11-13). Until it did, a sample was recognised across a corrected number or date only by its observation, and a trap sample entered in Beeline has none: renumbering one made the next full pass enter it as a second sample arriving, cut off from the entries under its old number, and entering one was recorded by the nightly under nobody's name. Production's ids have been permanent since cutover, so each snapshot row now has a `sample_id`, and a row whose id is a sample in the store is that sample's row whatever its reference has become. A pass narrowed to one sample follows its sample by id wherever its row sits, and enters a sample its caller says it has just made (`created`, which `POST /trap-samples/new` passes); a sample it cannot match it leaves to the full pass. The ids are one store's. The snapshot's first line names the store that wrote it, by the instant its first migration was recorded, with the last id it had handed out; a pass over a store with another identity, or one whose last id is lower (a store put back to an earlier copy, which would draw the same ids again), blanks the ids and matches as described above. That guard relies on the pass the app makes as it starts running before the restored store's ids catch up; ADR 0007's addendum says what happens when it does not, and that a dev store replaced by a newer copy needs its snapshot deleted. A snapshot written before this has no ids and is read as it stands: the first full pass after the deploy records what was pending and writes them, quiet or not. A release older than this one does not know the new header and re-baselines.

**Every entry carries the sample's id, and the History panel reads by it** (beeline-zytd, beeline-pe4b, [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-authored-changes-are-events.md)'s addendum of 2026-11-14). Each entry has two more cells, `store` and `sample_id`: the identity of the store that filed it and the sample's id there, taken from the store. A sample's history is the entries with its id (`sampleHistory`), so a swap of two numbers is two entries, a sample may stand on a reference another sample's row is still under, and a sample renumbered onto a number that a vanished sample once had gets none of that sample's entries. The rules `matchSamples` used to apply for a reader that followed references are gone. The reference is still filed on each entry, as the one the sample had before the pass. It is what joins a sample to the entries from before ids, which have ten cells and stay in the file as they are: a sample's first entry with an id names the reference its older entries are under, and a sample with no such entry is read at the reference it has now. Where two samples reach the same older entries neither is shown them. A sample entered as arriving (every field from nothing) is shown that pass and what follows. The parse of the log is indexed once and kept with it, so a sample page no longer folds the whole log: on production's log (136,674 entries, 2026-11-14) the index takes 82 ms to build and a history is then a lookup, and no sample's history differed from what the old reader showed. The snapshot may now hold two rows under one reference, a recorded sample's and the row of another that moved off it unrecorded. A line with the field `ids_restart` says a store's ids above a given one are being handed out again (a store put back to an earlier copy, or a snapshot deleted over a log with entries), and the reader leaves out what was filed before it under those ids. An entry is read only by the store that filed it, so a dev store rebuilt over a kept log shows only the entries from before ids, and a dev store replaced by a copy of production needs its log deleted along with its snapshot. A release older than this one skips the twelve-cell rows, and should not be rolled back to.

Two of the fields are staff decisions rather than facts about the bees: `hold`, the note of a hold that stands, and `waivers`, each flag waived on the sample with its reason and whether it has lapsed (beeline-plu, beeline-1kb.9), so a re-waive with a new reason and a lapse are both recorded. They are in the log so that the History panel says who held or waived, and when, which neither table records. Adding them changed the snapshot's columns, so the first pass after the deploy re-baselines, as it does after any new field.

**An applied upload is a source of its own** (beeline-kgyd.4.10). `SAMPLE_CHANGE_SOURCES` has `upload` beside `app` and `api`: the entry has an author, the staff member who applied the file, and its reason is "From upload N", so the history on the sample page names the file ("an uploaded file" where there is no author). The apply-uploads job enters the trap samples it made together, up to 25 in one narrowed pass (`recordTrapChanges`, `created: true`), because a narrowed pass rewrites the whole snapshot file whatever it names: on a copy of production's store of 19 November (11 MB of snapshot, 16 MB of log) one pass took about 0.65 s, which made 200 new samples take 138 s when each was entered alone and 14 s together (14.6 samples a second). **A deleted sample has no entry**: the log has no way to say that a sample is gone, since a sample that falls silent is recorded as nothing and every entry is a change to a `SAMPLE_FIELDS` field; deleting a trap sample ([Entering trap samples](entering-trap-samples.md#deleting-a-trap-sample)) is said in the server's log, and the sample's earlier entries stay in the file.
