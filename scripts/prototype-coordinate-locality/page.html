Localities from coordinates: a prototype

The question: can Beeline name a sample's locality from its coordinates well enough that volunteers stop having to fix iNaturalist's? A volunteer reads the locality to remember where they collected, or to find a specimen by its label, so it should name a town or peak they would know, in the same county as the point, and fit on the label (18 characters, no street address). A point inside a national wildlife refuge, or a WDFW wildlife area unit, is named for it. Change the settings and every number and row below is worked out again.

Throwaway (beeline-7i6). Towns, peaks and landmarks come from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0); counties and town limits from the 2023 Census boundaries; BC regional districts, municipalities and provincial parks from the province (ABMS, BC Parks); refuges from USFWS; national park units from the National Park Service; wildlife area units from WDFW; Washington state parks from WA State Parks; other parks and reserves from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the ODbL. The rows are samples from the sandbox store, with obscured and private observations left out. /*PRIVACY*/

How it does, by season

The current season is every sample since 1 March 2026. The past seasons are 4,000 samples at random, plus every one in a refuge, wildlife area unit, state or provincial park, or national park unit. "Agrees" means the generated name and the sample's present locality contain each other once spelling is folded (Mount/Mt, punctuation). The present locality is mostly iNaturalist's place guess, accepted onto a label, so agreement is a rough check against what staff have printed, not a measure of correctness.
