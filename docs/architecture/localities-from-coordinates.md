# Localities from coordinates

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#architecture-overview).

A sample's locality comes from iNaturalist: the first usable part of the observation's `place_guess`, which a volunteer or a geocoder wrote ([Ingestion](ingestion-jobs-and-elevation.md)). It is the commonest reason a label is flagged — too long, a street address, a postal town — and a volunteer clears the flag by guessing at a name that will pass. So Beeline names each sample's locality from its true coordinates as well, and offers that name beside the sample's own: on every sample page under the place, and on the front page on a locality flag's line (beeline-7i6). **It is a suggestion until somebody adopts it.** The sample's collector, someone acting for them, or staff can make it the sample's locality with one click (Peter, 2026-10-26, in place of copying it into iNaturalist). `POST /samples/:id/suggested-locality` makes the staff locality's write (`setStaffLocality`, beeline-649) credited to whoever clicked, so it lands in the sample overlay keyed by the observation, applies at once (the flag clears), stands over the observation on every sync, and is replayed after a rebuild; a sample with no observation takes it as a correction. The value is read from the store, never from the form, and a form showing a name the store no longer suggests is refused. The name is fixed once adopted: a later gazetteer changes the suggestion, not the sample. Whoever adopted it can go back to iNaturalist's locality, and nobody else: one a staff member set is a staff decision. Whether Beeline should become the authority for locality in general is the bead's step 3, undecided.

**The rule** is `src/locality-rule.ts`, a pure function from what the gazetteer says about a point to one name, ported from the prototype that Peter and Bonnie Zand reviewed (branch `prototype/coordinate-localities`). Its header lists the order: a refuge first of everything, because refuge specimens are handled separately (Peter, 2026-10-23); then a WDFW wildlife area unit, a National Park Service unit, a state or provincial park, a park or reserve OpenStreetMap names; a neighbourhood inside a city of 50,000 or more, else the town whose limits the point is in; a small island; a lake or pass that volunteers have already written for samples near it; a peak far from towns; a nearby town from the point's own county, then its own state or province, never across the line; a hamlet; a large wilderness; and last a distance and direction from the nearest town ("19km S Carcross"). On an island only places on the same island count, and a park is named only from inside it, because volunteers collect beside parks they have no permit for (Bonnie Zand, 2026-10-26). A name is shortened only as far as 18 characters needs, with USPS forms and the agencies' acronyms, and `fits()` is the label check restated: `test/locality-rule.test.ts` puts the localities `test/qc.test.ts` pins `qc_rule_locality_format` with to both, so the two cannot drift.

On the sandbox store (2026-10-26) it names 70,401 of 70,404 located samples; the three left are points in Nunavut, New Zealand and China. Against the prototype's own rule, run over the 20,788 samples its page carried, 99.3% get the same name; the rest are distances near a threshold, which the prototype rounded to 0.1 km before comparing, and two fixes made here: BC's "North Vancouver - City" is "North Vancouver", and a refuge named for a person goes by the surname, as staff write it (Finley NWR, not William L. Finley). Northern BC needed one more: at Pink Mountain the three nearest towns all give names too long for a distance ("121km SE Ft St. John"), so the last resort looks at the ten nearest.

**The gazetteer** is a separate DuckDB file, not tables in the store: about 190 MB of outlines and place names that a build reproduces from public sources, which has no business in the nightly backup. `pnpm locality:fetch` downloads the sources (`scripts/fetch-gazetteer.sh`: GeoNames, Census boundaries, BC's legal boundaries and parks, USFWS refuges, WDFW units, WA state parks, NPS units, and Geofabrik's OpenStreetMap extracts, about 2 GB); `pnpm locality:gazetteer` builds `data/gazetteer.duckdb` from them in about a minute (`src/build-gazetteer.ts`, `ingest/gazetteer.sql`, `ingest/gazetteer-osm.sql`). It is built on a workstation, because of the OpenStreetMap extracts, and goes up to the machine by hand ([the runbook](../runbooks/deploy-fly.md#the-gazetteer)). Each build has a fresh id in `gazetteer_build`. It must be built with the app's own `@duckdb/node-api`, never the `duckdb` command, whose version can differ. Its licences follow the data: GeoNames is CC BY 4.0 and OpenStreetMap the ODbL, so the file is ODbL too.

**The derive step** (`src/derive-locality.ts`) runs in the nightly after elevations, and by hand as `pnpm locality:derive`. It loads DuckDB's spatial extension, which the image installs at build time beside httpfs, attaches the gazetteer read-only, and names every sample that has no name yet, whose coordinates have moved from the point it was named at, or whose name came from another build — so uploading a new gazetteer renames everything on the next night. It works in batches of 5,000, each its own transaction, and the whole sandbox store took 77 seconds on a workstation. Like the elevation steps it never fails the run: with no gazetteer on the machine the run's detail says so. A name is a statement about a point, so `sample_locality_suggestion` (`schema/037`) records the point and the build, and `sample_locality_suggestion_current` (`schema/172`) is the one the pages read: a name for somewhere else is not shown. The lakes and passes that volunteers have named are read from the whole store on each run, so one sample's new locality does not rename its neighbours until something else makes them pending.

**Withheld where the location is.** The name says where the point is, so the sample page shows it only where the reader may see the coordinates (`location_withheld`, [One record](one-record.md)).

**Not adoptable once labels print.** A printed sample's locality is what its labels say (CONTEXT.md, *Upstream*), so the route refuses it. The sample page still shows the name, for judging it, with a line saying why; the front page leaves it off a printed sample's flags.
