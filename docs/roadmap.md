# Roadmap

Ordered build-out toward a **December 2026 cutover**. Vocabulary per [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md); the model being built is [schema-sketch.md](schema-sketch.md); open items that gate phases are in [questions.md](questions.md).

**Working stance until cutover:** databases are blown away and re-ingested freely; the schema is `schema/*.sql`, never a history of migrations. The one exception is a database nobody can rebuild — the sandbox, which becomes production at cutover — and [ADR 0006](adr/0006-migrations-for-deployed-stores.md) is how those catch up (`migrations/`, `pnpm db:migrate`). Corrections deliberately sit **outside** the blow-away path (the git-curated CSV and the app-written `data/corrections.csv`, [ADR 0004](adr/0004-correction-overlay.md)) so rebuilding never loses them; a dev environment that wants a truly clean slate deletes `data/corrections.csv` too. The person change log (`data/person-change.csv`, [ADR 0007](adr/0007-authored-changes-are-events.md)) sits outside it for a sharper reason still: a history the blow-away erases has nothing left to say about who changed anything.

## Phases

1. **Data model in SQL, against DuckDB.** Render the schema sketch into runnable DDL and views. QC rule views and printability are part of the model, not an app feature. Smoke-test the intended app toolchain (Kysely + DuckDB dialect) here, while switching is cheap. *Trap tables stay provisional pending the staff trap questions.*
2. **Legacy ingestion (MongoDB).** Single-shot in spirit, but re-runnable — it runs at least once more, at cutover. Includes taxonomy seeding: resolving verbatim determination names against the curated `animal` table (`ingest/parse-names.sql` takes the names apart; `legacy_name_parse` is the survey of what those rules do to every distinct string). **Acceptance test:** reconcile derived QC findings and record counts against production (`errorFlags`, ~38k flagged records).
3. **iNaturalist ingestion.** Authenticated sync (trusted coordinate reads), iterated until it looks right. Design the correction-overlay rule here even though it only matters post-cutover ([ADR 0004](adr/0004-correction-overlay.md)): corrections apply over the latest load; upstream converging to the corrected value auto-retires the correction; upstream moving to a third value leaves the correction standing and surfaces a `correction_conflict` QC finding.
4. **Web app.** Authenticated from the first page (iNat OAuth; no anonymous reads — `sample_true_location` makes leaks a live hazard). The self-service QC experience is the flagship. The app-store decision is made ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-app-process-owns-the-store.md)): the app process owns the DuckDB store, ingestion moves in-process under interactivity SLAs; stack is Hono + SSR with Lit islands. Plan: epic beeline-2c3.
5. **Label printing.** Design after the Arthur/Andony printing walkthrough (questions P1–P7). Mock exercises end to end: print runs, proofing pulls, reprints, mailing addresses — which Beeline keeps itself rather than reading from IdeaLogic — and the Avery mailing labels printed from them (beeline-1kb.6, beeline-1kb.16).
6. **Determinations UI.**
7. **Export / archiving.** For the time being Beeline exports nothing automatically, to Ecdysis or to GBIF: staff download specimen data and upload what they choose, and determinations come back from Ecdysis the same way (Andony, 2026-09-11; Peter, 2026-09-14). That choice is where each atlas's policy is applied — Caleb Lankford's review of volunteer determinations for Washington; for Oregon, Lincoln Best's judgement of the volunteer and his embargo — so the download carries volunteer determinations, marked as such, rather than leaving them out (beeline-pyr). A nightly dump in the legacy system's format keeps existing reporting working past cutover (beeline-6q8). Real backups start here — nightly, to the OSU/MM Google Drive, for recovery only (beeline-elf) — ahead of the first data that can't be regenerated.
8. **Cutover — December 2026.** Collecting is quiet by December (confirm from observation seasonality once the iNat pipeline is up); up to a week of downtime is acceptable. Freeze the legacy system, final Mongo pull into a fresh database, first real print run. The blow-away era ends: minted field numbers are forever, backups are mandatory, and the correction overlay goes live. What people need from Canvas to do their work moves over; Canvas itself stays (beeline-jn3). *Gated on the per-atlas geoprivacy answers and any other before-go-live questions.*

**April 2027 — the BLM surveys.** Olivia's collectors are to be trained and ready in April 2027, four months after cutover: three standardized protocols, a collecting event holding a day's samples and photos, field entry without connectivity, and signing up to a program that is not an atlas ([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md), Programs and governance; [questions](questions.md); the field-entry survey is [research/field-data-entry.md](research/field-data-entry.md)). The collecting event and a sample's start and end *times* are cheapest to model before cutover, while the schema can still be rebuilt rather than migrated; the entry tool and sign-up can follow it. For her protocols the entry interface is no longer unknowable, which is why the deferred item below is now scoped to the other atlases' traps.

**Deferred past cutover:** notifications/feed (derivable from determination and print-run events); trap-sample entry interface for Oregon's and Washington's multi-day traps — the BLM surveys' are known and scheduled above — (unknowable until the staff registry questions come back — until then iNat and the legacy import are the only data entry points); rebuilding the pivot tables staff use for an overview of recent atlas activity (beeline-4sl); supply requests, replacing the Google Form (beeline-bee).

## DuckDB vs PostgreSQL

Decided as [ADR 0001](adr/0001-duckdb-first-with-portable-sql.md): DuckDB first, behind dialect-neutral SQL, no ORM owning the schema, Kysely as the query layer. The phase-4 re-decision landed as [ADR 0005](adr/0005-app-process-owns-the-store.md): the app store stays DuckDB, the single app process owns it (ingestion becomes in-process scheduled jobs under interactivity SLAs, with a night-window carve-out), and Postgres remains the escape hatch if the SLAs prove unmeetable.
