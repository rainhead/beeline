# And to a sample

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

Samples get the same treatment (beeline-ewl, the second instance of [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-authored-changes-are-events.md)): `data/sample-change.csv` (`src/sample-change.ts`) is append-only events, produced by the same compare-against-what-was-last-said pass at the end of legacy promotion, after observation promotion, and at boot — so the nightly's coordinate rewrites, minting, free-linking, and a staff edit all leave a trace, and a writer that forgets is caught by the next pass. The edit screen runs a pass narrowed to its one sample, so the author is credited with their edit and nothing else. Where it deliberately differs from the person log: the baseline lives in `data/sample-state.csv` — one current row per sample, restated wholesale each pass — because the person log's in-log baseline over this corpus would be a million rows; the reference is the derived triple (collector as the overlay names them, number, start date), with the observation link recognising a sample across a corrected number or date — and outranking the triple where a sample carries a different observation from the one recorded and another sample now carries that one, which is what splitting two observations that shared a number leaves (a renumbered sample, and a newcomer on its old number) — and a triple collision (a live duplicate) recorded as nothing and counted. Two gaps in that matching were found by property testing before cutover (2026-10-30): the number-and-date tier now sees the samples on a shared triple, whose links and collectors are still the store's even though the pass cannot speak for them, so a stranger with the same number and date no longer inherits a colliding sample's row; and the edit screen's one-sample pass asks the store how many samples derive its triple (`tripleCountSql`), because it holds one sample and could not see that a renumbering had made it a duplicate, and then filed the edit as changes to the other sample's history. The record page reads it as the History panel on `/samples/:id`, from a parse the app takes at boot and extends with its own appends, since the log is 15 MB on the sandbox and re-parsing it after every nightly cost the day's first sample page about 1.5 s. Measured on the dev store: baseline 1.1 s and 11.2 MB, a steady pass ~2 s and zero entries, and a nightly-shaped 30 moves + 2 renumbers recording exactly 32. The snapshot is a statement about one store, and a full pass **refuses** one that mostly names samples the store does not hold — no entries, no restatement, and a message naming the file — because the vanishing rule records silence and a store that has never seen a sample records an arrival, so a scratch store pointed at the shared `data/` paths turns one boot into a million-row append on the next real pass (the screenshot fixture did exactly that on 2026-09-21, and the 1,085,665 rows appeared on 2026-09-23; beeline-hrw). Refusing rather than re-baselining is what makes the mistake cost nothing in either direction: the fixture's own boot leaves the real snapshot alone. A store that has genuinely replaced the snapshot's re-baselines by deleting the file, and a snapshot with no rows is read as no snapshot. On the nightly a refusal **fails the run**, after the elevation step so nothing the store needs waits on it, because a refusal is a standing condition that needs a person and a green `/healthz/jobs` over an unrecorded history would hide it; the CLIs and the boot pass warn. Start a scratch app with `BEELINE_SAMPLE_CHANGES`/`BEELINE_SAMPLE_STATE`/`BEELINE_PERSON_CHANGES` pointed away from `data/` regardless; the person log has no snapshot to protect and simply gains the fixture's people as arrivals.

**A trap sample entered in Beeline** (beeline-7r0g). A pass narrowed to one sample matches on the reference alone, so a sample whose number, start date or primary collector has just been corrected, and a sample that has just been made, are left to the next full pass. For a sample with an observation that costs only the author's name, because the full pass follows the link. A trap sample entered in Beeline has no observation and no corrections file: entering one recorded nothing until the nightly entered it under nobody's name, and the full pass after a renumbering recorded a second sample arriving, cut off from the entries under the old number, with the renumbering never an entry at all. So its two routes tell the pass what it cannot work out (`was` in `SampleRecordOptions`). `POST /trap-samples/new` says the sample is new, and it is entered as arriving under whoever entered it. `POST /samples/:id/trap` reads the sample's reference before the save (`sampleReference`), inside the same turn of the sample's queue, and the pass takes the snapshot row under that reference as the sample's. It takes the row only when no sample in the store still answers to the old reference and the snapshot has no row under the new one; otherwise a row may be another sample's, the pass records nothing, and the full pass decides, as before. No other route passes `was`: no other correction can move a reference.

Two of the fields are staff decisions rather than facts about the bees: `hold`, the note of a hold that stands, and `waivers`, each flag waived on the sample with its reason and whether it has lapsed (beeline-plu, beeline-1kb.9), so a re-waive with a new reason and a lapse are both recorded. They are in the log so that the History panel says who held or waived, and when, which neither table records. Adding them changed the snapshot's columns, so the first pass after the deploy re-baselines, as it does after any new field.
