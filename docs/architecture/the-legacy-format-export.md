# The legacy-format export

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#architecture-overview).

Reporting built on the legacy system's occurrences file — Andony's R comparison, the taxonomists' Ecdysis uploads — keeps working because Beeline writes that file too (`src/legacy-export.ts`, beeline-6q8, GitHub #107): every specimen, the legacy template's 63 columns in its order, csv-stringify's conventions (BOM, header, `\n`, quote only when needed, blank as nothing) and its composite_sort row order. The `legacy-export` job writes it nightly at 4am Pacific into `exportsDir`, and admins download it from `/exports`; DuckDB writes it with `COPY` because the corpus is ~160 MB. **Where a value comes from is a rule, not a column-by-column choice**: anything Beeline can change comes from the model — collectors, dates, places, coordinates, determinations, printing — so the file follows staff edits and Ecdysis determinations; what Beeline does not model at all (the legacy error flags, catalog numbers, resource links, the plant's higher taxonomy, sex on a specimen nobody has identified) is carried from the staged record of an imported specimen exactly, blanks included, and a Beeline-originated specimen gets the model's value where it holds the fact and a blank where it does not. The aim is to match the legacy file exactly (Peter, 2026-10-04), so a row describes one point: where it carries its own imported coordinates it carries that row's elevation and uncertainty too, never the sample's, since a sample that merged legacy rows taken at different points has one location and its elevation belongs to another place (beeline-en7i); a row likewise carries its own method, end date and place by name wherever the legacy rows of its sample disagree about them, since a sample holds one of each and one collector's sample 1 of 25 April 2019 was two specimens netted that day and three from pan traps emptied the next — read from the corrected rows, so a staff edit, which corrects every row of its sample, brings the model's value back, as does a locality override set on the sample; where Beeline holds no volunteer identification, the volunteer columns are the legacy row's, and where it holds no expert one, the expert columns are (a determiner with no name, `L.R.Best` on 88 records, has no determination to live on); and while the legacy determination is still the record its subgenus is the legacy row's, since the tree files a species under its genus and the subgenus the determiner stated is held nowhere else (Peter, 2026-10-04). Legacy vocabulary is kept where it differs from the model's: `CA`/`NZ` rather than ISO alpha-3, several collectors joined with `" | "` in the name columns as the register wrote a shared login, a qualifier inside `scientificName`. `pnpm legacy:compare-export` is the done-test: on the 2026-09-27 corpus the open season differs in a few hundred values per column out of 61,400 records, and the settled seasons mostly where Beeline normalised (Roman-numeral months, `Male`) or filled what older records left blank (the higher taxonomy of 158,669 identified specimens). During the parallel run it is also the list of what nobody has explained yet (beeline-en7i): each difference gets a kind from its two values alone (`filled`, `case`, `date_form`, `country_code`, `subgenus_form`, `authorship`, … or `changed`), or from what the store knows where the values cannot say it — `collector_alias` (the legacy spelling of a collector that `ingest/collector-aliases.csv` corrects), `taxon_alias` (the same for a genus or epithet and `ingest/taxon-aliases.csv`), `subgenus_form` again where a genus column holds only `(Peponapis)` and the tree files it under the genus exported, `staff_correction` (the legacy row as staff corrected it), `newer_determination` (an expert identification from Ecdysis or Beeline), `label_name`, `initial_from_surname`, `collector_order_contradicted` (a record whose recordedBy and name columns list its pair in opposite orders), `login_renamed` (the same iNaturalist user id under another login), `sample_disagreement` (promotion found the legacy rows merged into this specimen's sample disagreeing about that place field), a record only one side holds is a difference about the whole record kinded by why — the blocking promotion finding that stopped it, or whether Beeline minted its number — and `ingest/legacy-export-rulings.csv`, written by hand and reviewed in git, says which are explained: by column, by kind, or one record by field number, the most specific ruling winning, with the outcome (`expected`, `reference-wrong`, `beeline-wrong`, `known-gap`), why, and where the call was made. A `beeline-wrong` ruling that matches nothing any more reports itself retired, so a fixed defect never hides behind a stale ruling. The legacy side is the staged pull, which Beeline also imports from, so this measures what Beeline does to the legacy records rather than two independent systems, and the report names the pull by a fingerprint of its rows; it runs from a workstation after each pull, with no job and no gate, since the parallel run ends at cutover. Every difference, with the ruling that explains it, goes to `data/exports/legacy-export-differences.csv` for anyone auditing it — under `data/` because it names people.

`/exports` also lists a Darwin Core archive for each program and calendar year (`src/app/dwc-archive.ts` the format, `src/app/program-archives.ts` the writer, beeline-rvun): the specimens CSV's columns, tab-separated behind a `meta.xml` that declares only the terms, and every determination of each specimen in the Identification extension — the history `determination_of_record` flattens — with Symbiota's `identificationIsCurrent` (1 or 0) on each, since without it Symbiota imports every identification as not current. The `dwc-archives` job writes them nightly at 4am Pacific into `<exportsDir>/dwca/` beside a `manifest.json`, **every program-year with specimens, so staff can download and review them** (Peter, 2026-10-23; until then one was written only when its program had both a licence and a privacy policy in force for it, which with no policy yet meant none was). A year is the calendar year a sample was collected in, by its first day, because that is how the taxonomists count (Peter, 2026-10-23); since beeline-rxn8 that is also what a season is (CONTEXT.md), so an archive holds one season. Until then the season ran from 1 March and was judged by a sample's last day, and 266 samples on the dev store fell in different years in the archives and everywhere else. A year is ready to *publish* only when its program has both, and the page says how far each has got. Those are decisions curated in git, `ingest/program-licenses.csv` and `ingest/program-privacy-policies.csv` (`src/program-governance.ts`): each row holds from its year (`from_year`) until the program's next row and the latest holds indefinitely — Peter's presumption that a program continues with the licence it last used — so ranges cannot overlap, and a year before a program's first row has none. Licences are the three GBIF accepts, written into `meta.xml` as a constant `dcterms:license` where one is in force and left out where none is; the seeded rows are the licences each atlas's records were last published under on GBIF, with the dataset as the source. The privacy-policy file starts empty, so for now no year is ready to publish. Every program-year with specimens is listed with its count, licence and policy, read live rather than from the manifest, and a download of its archive; an archive whose year no longer has specimens is removed at the next run, and a manifest from before the archives were cut by year is read as no manifest. Which specimens are a program's follows CONTEXT.md's rule with no collecting events yet: an atlas's are those collected on its ground, Master Melittology's those no atlas covers. **The BLM surveys get no archive, and must never get one by geography**: their samples will arrive through their own pipeline (field entry, and the collecting event that says whose day it was), so a BLM sample from New Mexico is BLM's, not the New Mexico Bee Atlas's — when that lands, the archives select by the sample's program rather than by where it fell. The page says not to upload them: which identifier Oregon's records are published under, OSAC's or none (beeline-1kb.22; Beeline mints no `occurrenceID`, per ADR 0008's second amendment), each atlas's answer on taxon-obscured coordinates (beeline-1kb.7.1), and how Beeline holds a publication embargo on particular records (beeline-bqvp) are undecided. A year is the grain Beeline produces and checks at, not the grain to publish: a program should publish one dataset that grows, since a dataset per year is republished by the next cumulative one — every record of Oregon's 2018 and 2019 datasets is on GBIF twice — and GBIF gives a dataset one licence ([docs/research/program-governance-for-exports.md](../research/program-governance-for-exports.md), which also holds what a privacy policy must answer and how others record an embargo). Which volunteer determinations go downstream is not among them: each atlas's staff decide that after downloading, outside Beeline (beeline-pyr). **Beside each program's years is its checklist** (`src/app/dwc-checklist.ts`, beeline-024a), every year together: Darwin Core has no Taxon extension for an occurrence archive to carry, so the names are a second archive with Taxon as its core, one per program because a checklist says what somebody recorded (Peter, 2026-10-26). It holds the names the program's specimens are identified to by determination of record, the identification the occurrence archive carries, with their ancestors so the classification is whole, in `/taxonomy`'s order and with kingdom to genus on each row; `taxonID` and `parentNameUsageID` are `entity_id`s, by the occurrence archives' rule. Ecdysis's id, its standing and the name it files a synonym under are Beeline's own columns rather than `taxonomicStatus`, since the tree is the program's classification and a name Ecdysis calls a synonym is still the name the specimens carry; so are the program's specimens at and below each name, how many of them an expert identified, and the first and last year. It states no licence, because licences are decided a year at a time and a checklist spans every year. Its `id` is the specimen's `entity_id`, which joins the two files and which Symbiota keeps as the record's key; a rebuild redraws it, but only the sandbox is rebuilt, and no archive the sandbox writes will be uploaded anywhere (Peter, 2026-10-10).
