## BLM surveys and programs (ask Olivia; two are BLM's or the Logan bee lab's to answer through her)

From Peter's meeting with Olivia, 2026-09-17; what she told us is in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md) under Programs and governance. Her volunteers are to be trained and ready in April 2027, and the options for field data entry are surveyed in [research/field-data-entry.md](research/field-data-entry.md).

1. **Her data model.** She keeps the surveys in an Access database. Peter has seen its table diagram (2026-09-17), and it answers some of what follows and sharpens the rest; what is still wanted is a handful of real rows from each table. What the diagram shows: a **day-place** (a location on a date, with the plot's plants and their abundance hanging off it) holds several **collection events** (one method, a start and stop time, up to three collectors, a project, a bee count, the weather), which hold **specimens** keyed by a barcode. **The words are swapped against ours**: her collection event is Beeline's sample, and her day-place is Beeline's collecting event. Locations are reusable, with their own id, coordinates, habitat and a shorthand; plants are USDA PLANTS symbols; a specimen carries one species; collectors carry email, institution and phone; and a project table stands where we have been saying program.
2. **The sample identifier.** Access assigns one when a record is processed. What does it look like, does it print on a label, and do BLM survey specimens get Beeline field numbers like everyone else's? And where are the labels mailed — to the collector, as an atlas's are, or to whoever pins for the surveys? Beeline will not print labels it has no address to send to (Peter, 2026-09-24).
3. **Before and after.** The site code is BLM's and we treat it as opaque. Her diagram suggests the answer to how she ties this year's visit to last year's: a reusable location with its own id. Is the location's shorthand the BLM code, and does one code ever cover more than one location?
4. **One sample, two programs.** A BLM protocol sample taken in New Mexico is inside the New Mexico Bee Atlas's ground. Is it an atlas record, a BLM survey record, or both — and does the answer change who sees it or where the bees go? *(Answered on Beeline's side, Peter 2026-09-24: both, as two facts — the atlas derived from where it fell, the program stated. Still hers: whether that changes who sees it or where the bees go.)*
5. **Signing up.** How does somebody become a BLM survey collector, what separates a volunteer from a contractor in practice, and does BLM need to be told who they are?
6. **Rare plants.** Which plants would need their locations withheld, and who says — a state list, BLM's, hers? Does withholding cover every sample at the plot, or only the record of the plant?
7. **What BLM's access looks like.** They want access and not ownership, and have no view on how. What would actually satisfy them — a sign-in, a yearly export, a report? *(Narrowed, Peter 2026-09-24: BLM will not use the field tool; whatever it gets comes from Beeline or downstream of it. The form it takes is still hers to say.)*
8. **What the Logan bee lab wants to know about volunteers, and why.** Attribution on a specimen is a name, which is already public. Contact details and whereabouts are a different thing: Beeline holds no email or mailing address for anyone yet, and would want the volunteer's say-so recorded before passing either on.
Her draft documents — the BLM narrative and the monitoring protocol, 2026-09-17 — are read against this list in [research/blm-protocol-notes.md](research/blm-protocol-notes.md), which says for each question what they answer. They refine the meeting rather than contradict it, and the glossary follows them; what the refinements leave to ask:

## Taxon geoprivacy (⚠ blocker: Oregon has answered; Washington next)

Raised by Nora (2026-08-19), and asked on [gh-13](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/issues/13). iNaturalist obscures coordinates for sensitive taxa regardless of the collector's wishes, and each atlas sits in its own regulatory environment, so this is not a decision Beeline can make on every atlas's behalf. Beeline holds **both** the public (possibly obscured) and the true coordinates, so any answer is implementable; what is open is *revelation*, per atlas. In this program the observation is of the host plant, so a taxon-obscured record is one whose plant iNaturalist treats as rare, and the question is about protecting plant populations (Peter, gh-13, 2026-09-17).

**Oregon answered on 2026-10-21** ([Arthur, gh-13](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/issues/13#issuecomment-6327141377)), and the answer to question 3 holds for every atlas that has not:

1. **Labels and exports.** Oregon's labels may carry the true coordinates. Downstream, what Oregon protects is the plant, not the point: whether a record goes out with its host plant is Lincoln Best's call at upload, so many of Oregon's GBIF records carry a bee at a rare plant's exact coordinates with no plant named. Somebody who asks for the full bee and plant data can be given it, rare plant and exact point included.
2. **What binds Oregon.** Nothing: no legislation and no iNaturalist term stops it publishing a rare plant's true coordinates. Collectors can choose to share exact coordinates with the program, and the program is free to choose whom it shares them with.
3. **Until an atlas answers, its taxon-obscured records stay unprintable.** Beeline now does this: `taxon_obscured_awaiting_atlas` blocks a taxon-obscured sample unless its atlas is in `atlas_taxon_obscured_printing` ([schema/010](../schema/010_people_atlases.sql)), where Oregon is the only row. A sample outside every atlas has no atlas to answer for it, so it waits too. Before this, a taxon-obscured sample printed as soon as Beeline held its true point; the question had called that the current behaviour, and it never was (beeline-1kb.7).
4. **Other atlases' staff** may see Oregon's true coordinates for its rare plants.

**Still open, for every other atlas:** the same four questions. Washington is the one that matters soonest, because Oregon prints Washington's labels: 4 open-season Washington samples on the dev store stopped printing on this answer. The people to ask are those who upload Washington's records, Caleb Lankford and Karen Wright. Within the program, a collector and their own atlas's staff already see true coordinates in the app and its CSV exports (Peter, 2026-08-23), because that is a participant reading their own records rather than revelation. Whether one atlas's staff may see another's is answered only for Oregon's records; if any atlas says no, staff reach has to narrow by program (beeline-wf3n). Downstream stays in staff's hands: Beeline exports nothing automatically, and the nightly archives are for staff to review, not to publish, until a program has a privacy policy (beeline-rp33), which is where Oregon's answer about the host plant belongs.

## Printing and mailing (what is left is Arthur's, bar the last)

Arthur has now answered nearly all of this section — the proofing and stock questions on 2026-09-08; on 2026-09-09 what pulls a record from a run (nothing ever has), who swaps a superseded label (nobody confirms it), and whether a lost-label reprint renumbers (it does not); and on 2026-10-21, on [gh-17](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/issues/17#issuecomment-6327562233), the three that had been waiting a month: a bad-data reprint keeps its number, nobody needs the swap confirmed, and the 2018 identifiers were printed on the pins. The practice answers are recorded in [reference-implementation.md](reference-implementation.md) under *The printing moment, as the operator runs it*; the numbering rule is [ADR 0008](adr/0008-specimen-identity.md), accepted on the strength of the 2026-10-21 answer.

What is left is where the old duplicate field numbers lived (question 6), which is Arthur's and was asked again the same day, and the two questions that are for the museums rather than for staff (questions 4 and 7).

Two older questions went for reasons other than being answered. Q3 (how other atlases plan to print and mail) was never Arthur's: no other atlas currently prints or mails (Peter, 2026-08-28), so it is future scope. Q7 (attribution for records belonging to no member atlas) is dropped as confused rather than open — a label has six fields and carries no atlas or program name at all, so there is no slot for the question it asked; the reasoning is on [gh-16](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/issues/16), closed, and the residue is an eventual Ecdysis/GBIF `institutionCode` that follows physical custody rather than membership.

1. **Which account of the bad-data reprint is right?** *Answered.* Andony's. Arthur, 2026-10-21: when the data is wrong, the record is corrected and a new label for the same record is printed with the corrected data, and both should be visible afterwards — the record's edit history, and the print history of the original label and of the reprint. So a field number is minted once and every reprint reuses it, which is what [ADR 0008](adr/0008-specimen-identity.md) decided and what the print-run model already does; the ADR moves from proposed to accepted on this answer. The renumbering Arthur had described on 2026-09-08 was the duplicate-repair case, where two labels already carry one number. What his answer asks of Beeline — a reprint's history beside the original's — is beeline-1kb.5's to deliver: `printed_label` already keeps one row per label per run, and the sample's History panel the edits.
2. **Does anyone want the label swap confirmed?** *Answered: no.* Today nobody confirms it: the volunteer does the swap if the problem is found before they submit their bees, the label czar or another lab member if after, and the only backstop is that a mismatch surfaces when bees are scanned for determinations (Arthur, 2026-09-09). Asked whether Beeline should record the swap, Arthur said a checkbox on the specimen or sample page could do it, but the case is rare and staff are not sure it is worth doing (2026-10-21). So Beeline does not build it; a reprint's own history shows that a replacement was printed, and that is as far as the record goes.
3. **What should a shared label say?** *Answered.* Both names, always: Andony said on gh-17 (2026-09-15) that the O'Loughlins' trap samples should carry both, and the form was decided in the room on 2026-09-18, going through the first print run — set tight with a shared surname said once, `M.&D.O'Loughlin` ([#79](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/pull/79), [src/label-text.ts](../src/label-text.ts)). The legacy code had produced artifacts rather than a decision — `M. and D.O'Loughlin` (10,984 specimens), `M. | D.O'Loughlin | O'Loughlin` (10,515). Kept here, numbered, because the other questions are cited by number.
4. **If a number is retired after publication, how do Ecdysis and GBIF learn of it?** Much smaller than it was, and smaller again since 2026-10-21: no reprint renumbers, so the only renumbering Beeline will ever do is a duplicate repair. [ADR 0008](adr/0008-specimen-identity.md) keeps `occurrenceID` still, so that repair is an `otherCatalogNumbers` entry rather than a retirement, and the question survives only as "what do we send the museum" for that case.
5. **Were the 2018 name-based identifiers printed verbatim on physical labels?** *Answered: yes.* Arthur, 2026-10-21: the `First_Last:18.sss.nnn` identifiers were printed on the pinned labels. (Andony's earlier answer, that the 2018 labels predate the unique identifier and that identifiers are composite keys derived from label elements, had read as *no*; it was about how the database wrote them down, not about the pins.) The `E` half was already answered: it denotes Emily Carlson's PhD work (Andony, gh-17). So every one of the five identifier eras in [field-number-history.md](field-number-history.md) is on a pin somewhere, and a number read off an old label can be any of them.
6. **Where did the historical cross-project duplicate field numbers live** — this database, Ecdysis, or between them — and do the **physical** labels still collide? Andony says duplicate resolution is underway, 2025 first and then back through 2024 (gh-17), which answers that they are being dealt with but not where they were or what happens to the pins. Asked again of Arthur on gh-17, 2026-10-21; it is the last thing open there.
7. **Will Ecdysis accept an `occurrenceID` we supply, and keep deriving `WSDA_<field number>`?** New with [ADR 0008](adr/0008-specimen-identity.md). Symbiota mints its own UUID by default; if Beeline is to be the source of the persistent identifier, WSUC has to agree to carry ours. And separately: has anything in the 598 records with a duplicated `occurrenceID` already been published (beeline-1kb.14)? *Answered 2026-10-01 from OSAC's published archive: no duplicated identifier is in it, and the one specimen of the 598 that is published went out under its own number — see ADR 0008, open question 3.*

## Taxonomy across the atlases

Raised 2026-08-27 by Peter: the atlases disagree about taxonomy to some degree. Whether Beeline ever models that divergence is [beeline-0zb](../.beads/), and the stance there is to defer until a second opinion is actually being read — but the shape of the disagreement decides whether that is a curation conversation or a schema.

1. **Where do the atlases, or the outside experts they hand specimens to, depart from ITIS?** Narrowed on 2026-09-11: the program's basis is ITIS, for bees and bycatch, and volunteers determine against it (Andony). So the question is no longer which backbones are in play but which departures are. A handful of contested genera is a curation problem; an atlas on a different backbone would not be.
2. **Does the disagreement change what goes on a label, or only how records are grouped for analysis?** A label prints one name — those are different problems with different answers.
3. **When two atlases disagree, whose name reaches Ecdysis and GBIF?** Related to the attribution question for records belonging to no member atlas, which is closed as confused rather than open ([gh-16](https://github.com/rainhead/beeline/issues/16)).
4. **Do determiners use identification qualifiers beyond `cf.`, `aff.` and `nr.`?** Those three are what `determination.qualifier` now takes ([beeline-tgu](../.beads/)) — the ones that modify a species assertion and cannot be said by dropping to genus. The legacy corpus attests only `nr.`, three records. If determiners also want `sp. nov.` or `s. str.` recorded rather than derived from rank, that is a wider vocabulary and a change to the CHECK. Not a printing question: a label carries no taxon at all ([reference-implementation.md](reference-implementation.md)), so this is about what reaches Ecdysis and GBIF.
