# CONTEXT.md — Domain Language

Shared vocabulary for Beeline. Use these terms as defined here, in code, commits, and conversation. Update this file whenever a term is coined, sharpened, or deprecated.

Beeline serves the Master Melittologist program (OSU Extension) and its member atlas projects. It has two jobs: it is the **authority for cataloging specimens** collected by atlas members and recording their taxonomic **determinations**, and it **governs the printing of specimen labels**. Downstream systems (reporting, GBIF, Ecdysis) consume its records. Beeline is the program's **internal** tool, for people already in it; the program's domain `melittologist.org` is expected in time to carry a **public** tier as well — what the atlases are, and how to join them. Two scopes, one domain ([docs/surrounding-systems.md](docs/surrounding-systems.md)). It replaces the reference implementation ([OBP-Server](https://github.com/oregon-bee-project/OBP-Server/), informally also "Beeline"); see [docs/reference-implementation.md](docs/reference-implementation.md) for what that system actually does.

## Core objects

- **Specimen** — one physical insect collected for the program: usually a bee, occasionally a fly or wasp. The unit that gets a field number, a label, and determinations. **Specimen records are individuated by printing**: until a print run freezes, a sample carries only a count, free to move up or down; the print run creates the specimen records, mints their field numbers, and prints their labels. Nothing needs a specimen record before that moment. The reference implementation calls its specimen row an *occurrence* (Darwin Core flavor); we say specimen for the thing and reserve occurrence-speak for exports.
- **Bycatch** — non-bee specimens (flies, wasps, …) collected incidentally and catalogued like any other specimen. Small in number but genuinely valuable to science; some staff actively identify it (Ben Lee, wasps).
- **Sample** — the collecting event that yields specimens: one collector, one place, one floral host (or no host), one day (net) or one date range (trap). A collector's net samples for a day are numbered 1, 2, 3, … (the **sample number**). Specimens within a sample are numbered 1..N (the **specimen number**). A sample's specimen count can grow over time — trap collectors process large catches in batches, and net collectors correct counts — so "how many specimens does this sample have?" has a time-dependent answer.
- **Net sample** — bees netted in one place on one day, usually off one floral host, documented by an iNaturalist observation (of the plant, or a blank/subjectless observation for bees collected off no flower). The observation carries the sample number and specimen count as observation field values.
- **Trap sample** — contents of a trap collected on a date; specimens are attributed to the date *range* since the previous servicing, not a single day. Recorded by a small number of volunteers/staff/grad students, but not small in volume: ~52k production records (14%) are trap-collected, with prefixed sample series (`OBAS-00657`, `WBAS-…`) and single samples of 2,000+ specimens. Currently spreadsheet-based; needs a first-class interface. Protocol is free text today ("vane trap"/"vane traps"/"6 Vane Traps"/"pan traps"/"trap nest"/"vacuum"), sometimes smuggling effort counts into the protocol string. *(Open: most of this subdomain — see [docs/questions.md](docs/questions.md).)*
- **Observation** — a record on iNaturalist. For Beeline, an observation is *evidence of a sample*: its taxon is the floral host, its OFVs carry sample number and specimen count, its coordinates/date locate the sample. Beeline records which iNat project(s) an observation belonged to as provenance only — atlas assignment is geographic. The per-atlas projects persist for now, alongside a catch-all ([Master Melittologist (outside of Oregon)](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/master-melittologist-outside-of-oregon)).
- **Floral host** — the plant a sample was collected from, identified by the sample's iNat observation. Referenced as an iNaturalist taxon (see Taxonomy).
- **Collector** — the person who collected a sample: a volunteer or staff member of an atlas, or of the program itself (see Membership), identified on iNaturalist by their login. The reference implementation keeps collectors in a CSV and has no user accounts; Beeline intends collectors to be real users who sign in (via iNaturalist OAuth).
- **Atlas** (project) — a member program: Oregon Bee Atlas, and the atlases in BC, Washington, Idaho, New Mexico, Oklahoma, under the Master Melittologist umbrella. Samples are assigned to atlases **by geography**, never by which pipeline or iNat project they arrived through (multiple iNat projects confuse volunteers who visit each other's projects). Each atlas names its iNaturalist *place* (Washington = place 46); since iNat stamps every observation with its place ids, assignment is a lookup we never have to compute ourselves. An atlas that prints its own labels gets a flag for that, and only particular people may print. Staff know each other across atlases — a high-trust environment — so atlas-level roles stay minimal until a need appears. Geography won't always decide: GPS accuracy can make a border observation ambiguous, and some collecting happens where no atlas exists (handled ad hoc today — e.g. Arizona bees processed through Washington because of a personal relationship). When geography doesn't decide, assignment to an atlas for processing is an explicit administrative — possibly volunteer — action, not an inference. This is a real population, not an edge case: production holds ~2,500+ records from at least nine states/provinces outside the six atlases (Nevada 1,238; Kansas 291; Montana, California, Yukon, Arizona, …). Collecting there is ordinary rather than a failure, and the model says so positively: every US state and Canadian province is known, and the ones no atlas covers say so, so "outside the atlases" is never confused with a place that did not resolve. Most such records are atlas members travelling, not program-only volunteers — the two are different questions and are asked separately. *(The reference implementation has no atlas entity at all; records carry only `stateProvince`.)*
- **Membership** — where a person belongs: a member atlas, or **Master Melittology itself, with no atlas**. The second is a real state, not missing data — a Nevada volunteer works under OBA staff's auspices without being an Oregon Bee Atlas volunteer, and iNat project [Master Melittologist (outside of Oregon)](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/master-melittologist-outside-of-oregon) (99706, Andony's) is how the program already runs it. Membership is editorial: staff set it, and it is never inferred from where someone's samples fell — a Washington volunteer collecting in Oregon is still WaBA. It is also distinct from **administration**, which is who staffs them; for the program-only case that is OBA staff, and until there is a second administering body the two are not worth telling apart. Roughly a dozen people in the store belong here, against ~160 nobody has been asked about yet — so "no atlas recorded" and "no atlas applies" have to stay separable answers (beeline-lcl). *(The reference implementation has no notion of membership at all.)*
- **Determination** — a person asserting a taxon (and sex/caste) for a specimen. Determinations are **append-only events**: each records who, what, when, and the channel it arrived through, and a specimen's determination of record is computed from them. Two channels, and both stay live: **expert determinations** (authoritative; some staff — Caleb Lankford in Washington — intend to keep identifying in Ecdysis, arriving as CSV round-trips) and **volunteer determinations** (the collector's own identification, never overwriting an expert's). Determinations routinely arrive one, two, or three years after collection, and in bursts: taxonomists work in batches by morphospecies, so a collector may see two dozen identifications land at once for specimens spanning multiple seasons. Difficult groups and bycatch are handed on to outside experts (e.g. many *Dialictus* go to Joel Gardner at WSU). *(Open: whether experts also determine in Beeline directly; and the interaction design for volunteer determinations — momentary data-entry mistakes shouldn't become permanent history, which suggests a draft/commit boundary where only deliberate assertions become events.)*

## Custody

The specimen's physical journey, whose stages the model should be able to name even where it doesn't yet record them:

- **Labeling** — printed labels reach the collector by mail 1–4 weeks after collection; the collector pins their specimens and attaches the labels. Some collectors then attempt their own determinations, mostly to genus.
- **Turnover** — the collector hands or mails their season's specimens to their atlas's taxonomist, other staff, or a relaying volunteer, between season's end (~September) and the annual **Melittologist conference** (Corvallis, early March), when they're due. Relay points are informal — a front porch in Seattle serves as a dropbox.
- **Repository** — where specimens ultimately rest, varying by atlas: Washington → [WSUC](https://museum.entomology.wsu.edu/) (though taxonomists may hold some for reference or teaching); Oregon → OSAC, in boxes as lab space runs out; BC has no institutional sponsor (specimens accumulate with Bonnie Zand); Idaho and Oklahoma have institutional partners; New Mexico is Olivia's project. Knowing where a specimen physically *is* — and equipment, teaching supplies — is a natural future featureset, not core scope.

## Data handling

Which of these an attribute is decides how a wrong value gets fixed — and telling a volunteer the wrong one is how a screen becomes confusing. The axis is *who may change it*, and there are three answers. Per-column policy and per-row provenance are being written down as a catalog (beeline-bla).

- **Upstream** — data whose authority lives outside Beeline: iNaturalist observations, and the frozen legacy records. Beeline holds a projection and never edits it in place; a wrong value is fixed at the source (clearing on the next sync) or covered by a recorded correction ([ADR 0004](docs/adr/0004-correction-overlay.md)). Distinct from **downstream** — Ecdysis, GBIF, reporting — which consumes what Beeline publishes. Not "third-party data": that names who supplied it rather than who may change it, it excludes the legacy dump (the program's own former system, not a third party), and it collides with downstream, which is third-party too.
- **Derived** — computed from other data and owned by nobody: QC flags, printability, determination of record, atlas assignment, elevations, `sample.kind` from the presence of a date range. Not editable, because it isn't an assertion — a wrong value means a wrong input or a wrong rule. **A rebuild reproduces it exactly, which is the test.**
- **Authored** — what Beeline is the authority for, because it exists nowhere else: field numbers and specimen records, which it *mints*; determinations, corrections, memberships and staff decisions, which it *records*. A rebuild must not recompute these, which is why the correction store and the person overlay live outside the blow-away path ([roadmap](docs/roadmap.md)).

Two consequences worth stating, because both are already load-bearing:

- **Provenance often varies per row, not per column.** `sample.atlas_id` is derived from geography on most rows and authored on a few; `locality` is upstream on most and authored on the corrected ones. Where it varies, a **sibling column** carries the per-row answer — `sample.atlas_assigned_by`, `sample_location.source` and `elevation_source_id`, `determination.channel`, `person_admin.granted_by`. The siblings differ in shape on purpose: one is a trust level, one a citation, one an author.
- **There are two authored-change mechanisms, and this vocabulary says why.** Corrections carry `base_value` and merge three-way because they author over *upstream* data, which can move underneath them. The person overlay has none, because it authors over *derived* data, recomputed from scratch each rebuild. Neither is a history; making authored changes append-only **events** — the shape `determination` already uses — is beeline-o22.

## Identifiers

- **Field number** — the Beeline-issued per-specimen number printed on the label: currently a 2-digit-year prefix plus zero-padded sequence (e.g. `25000001`). Assigned only once a record is clean (no QC flags). Guaranteeing its uniqueness is one of Beeline's core jobs. The reference implementation calls it `fieldNumber` too and does *not* guarantee uniqueness (advisory max-scan, no unique index). **This is not the catalog number**: that name belongs to the museum's identifier (below), and calling ours by it is the confusion this entry exists to end (beeline-nfo, decided 2026-08-23). The store spells it `specimen.field_number`. Going forward, a single global sequence suffices (one production deployment) — but operator error a few years ago produced duplicate numbers *across* projects, so history is not globally unique. Either (atlas, field number) is the compound key forever, or historical data is handled separately from working data (current or current+prior season). Production data (surveyed 2026-08-18, 383k records) shows **four identifier eras**: 2018 name-based ids (`Jan_Knapik:18.001.001`, ~18.5k records), 7-digit numbers (`1800001`–`2463721`), 1,400 `E`-prefixed numbers (`E2000000`–`E2332481`), and 8-digit numbers (`25000001`+) — plus one live duplicate pair (`25051768`, printed twice). Any numbering decision must accommodate all four. *(Open: which approach — deserves its own think-through; ADR when decided. Also: what does the `E` prefix denote?)*
- **Catalog number / museum identifiers** — downstream identifiers derived from the field number: Ecdysis `catalogNumber` (e.g. `WSDA_2303966` for Washington) and OSAC occurrence URIs (`https://osac.oregonstate.edu/OBS/OBA_<number>`, Oregon only). The reference implementation's `catalogNumber` column means the *museum's* number, not Beeline's — and so does the word, here and in the volunteer-facing glossary.

## Taxonomy

Two taxonomic authorities, split by *role* (not by clade):

- **Curated taxonomy** — the bee taxonomy for which the program is itself the authority: a first-class, versioned entity, shared by all atlases. Curated by the program's taxonomists — Lincoln Best today; Karen Wright, Nate Green, August Jackson, Bonnie Zand, and Olivia Messinger-Carrill may all participate. Every **specimen determination** points into the curated taxonomy, so it also carries whatever non-bee nodes are needed to place **bycatch** — being in it is a consequence of playing the specimen role, not of being a bee. Empirically (WABA's Ecdysis data, 46k records, surveyed 2026-08-18): ~2.8% of family-determined specimens are non-bee, across ~25 families — mostly aculeate wasps, plus bee flies and beetles — and their determinations regularly reach genus and species; another ~1,300 records sit at coarse ranks (Hymenoptera, Diptera, Ichneumonoidea, Symphyta, Heteroptera), so the taxonomy must also admit determinations at suborder/superfamily ranks. *(Open: what to base the non-bee portion on — the GBIF backbone is a candidate seed.)*
- **iNaturalist taxonomy** — consumed directly for angiosperms. Every **floral host** reference points at an iNaturalist taxon. Host identifications improve over time — often a genuine correction, sometimes a year or more after observation — and Beeline wants those updates. Labels do not carry the host, so late host corrections never invalidate printed labels; propagation questions concern only our records and downstream exports. *(Open: how far and when corrections propagate; how to handle upstream taxon swaps/merges — likely store the iNat taxon id plus the name as observed.)*

## Label governance

- **Print run** — the batch act of printing, and a first-class entity with states **prepared → approved → printed → mailed** (mailed is marked by hand). The printer decides "I'm ready to print now" and *freezes* the set of printable records at that moment — a batch-mindedness from the reference system worth preserving — then proofs the dataset and/or PDF, possibly flagging records out by hand, prints, cuts the sheet apart by collector (labels sort by collector, then field number), and mails (Arthur: most Mondays April–September, via OSU's bulk mail). A print run snapshots what it printed. Cadence is the printer's choice, never dictated — but forecastable, so collectors can be told "your labels print Monday."
- **Label** — the physical pinned-specimen label: locality, coordinates+elevation, date+sample.specimen numbers, collector, method (net/trap/nest), field number, and a DataMatrix barcode. Printed on 250-per-sheet PDF pages by staff and mailed to the collector. Once printed, a label's content is immutable fact — bad data detected later means identifying and reprinting affected labels, not editing history.
- **Printable** — a specimen record qualifies for label printing: all label-required fields present, no blocking QC flags, and coordinates not obscured. Only printable records receive field numbers.
- **Reprint request** — a collector's report that a physical label needs reprinting — lost, destroyed, never attached — with the reason. Reprints are a normal part of life, not an error path; Beeline provides the channel and keeps the history.
- **Post-print trouble** — upstream changes after a label is printed. A deleted observation blocks any further printing for its specimens and goes to staff to investigate (the right response depends on why). A specimen count that *decreases* after printing is flagged and recorded — there may be nothing to do beyond knowing the label may never be attached to anything.
- **QC flag** — a named data-quality issue attached to a record (missing field, suspect locality, coordinate uncertainty > 250 m, obscured coordinates, non-plant host, …). Flags block printing and drive the self-service QC experience: users should see their flagged records, understand exactly what to do, and resolve them in one session — replacing the reference implementation's weekly hand-assembled emails.
- **Staff override** — an explicit, visible action replacing volunteer-supplied or upstream data: cleanup after an abrupt volunteer dropout (when we can no longer ask them to fix iNaturalist), assigning an out-of-region sample to an atlas, or accepting a machine-proposed rewrite (e.g. conforming a locality string to the rules instead of bouncing it back to the volunteer). Never silent: the override and its author are recorded, and what was overridden stays visible.

## Coordinates & privacy

- **Obscured coordinates** — iNaturalist geoprivacy (user-chosen or taxon-driven) shifts public coordinates. Two distinct concerns. *Accuracy*: Beeline must never mistake obscured coordinates for true ones, so obscured records are flagged and unprintable unless we hold the true coordinates (via project trust + authenticated reads, with provenance tracked per record). *Secrecy*, for taxon-driven obscuring: whether an atlas may reveal true coordinates of a sensitive taxon — on labels, and in exports that leave the program — depends on that atlas's policies and regulatory environment. **Within** the program this is settled (2026-08-23): a collector sees their own true coordinates and their own atlas's staff see them too, in the app and in its CSV exports, because that is a participant reading their own records rather than revelation; whether one atlas's staff may see another's is pending with Andony ([questions](docs/questions.md)). Beeline holds **both** coordinate pairs so either answer is implementable, and both live in the **main** store, not the private one — the private store protects *participants* (tokens, addresses), whereas a taxon-obscured coordinate protects a *plant population*, and anyone trusted with the main store is trusted to protect those too ([ADR 0003](docs/adr/0003-private-data-store.md), beeline-2yh). The reveal question is per-atlas, **open, and must be answered before go-live** ([questions](docs/questions.md)).
- **Collection locations are not private** — as a matter of *collector* privacy. Collecting for the program implies willingness to tell us — and the world — where, even if it was the collector's yard. The remedy for regret is scrapping the sample before handing specimens over or entering determinations. Observation-load history therefore retains coordinates without redaction — retention is unconditional; *revelation* of taxon-obscured coordinates is the separate open question above. *(Sanity-check against iNaturalist's obscured-coordinate terms when the trusted-read integration is built.)*
- **Mailing address** — the one truly private datum: printed labels are mailed to collectors, so the label-printing side of the system holds addresses, collectors can update their own, and nothing else needs access.

## Ecosystem

- **iNaturalist** — observation platform; source of samples (via project pulls), floral-host identifications, and (planned) user sign-in. Not event-sourced: ingestion must actively detect edits and deletions or silently drift.
- **Ecdysis** — the entomological collections database (Symbiota). **Per-atlas, not universal**: Washington uses it because WSUC does (and probably BC); Oregon does not use it at all. Regarded as janky; only a fraction is used, and absent the WSUC requirement it could be scrapped. Where used, it is both a consumer of Beeline records and the channel through which expert determinations return (CSV keyed by the museum's catalog number) — and the route by which Washington data reaches GBIF.
- **OSAC** — Oregon State Arthropod Collection, the identifier authority for Oregon specimens and Oregon's repository.
- **GBIF** — global biodiversity database. Two different paths: Washington data arrives via Ecdysis; Oregon publishes manually, and only after the **publication embargo**.
- **Publication embargo** — Oregon's policy of publishing data only once it has been around long enough for any grad student who wants to publish findings from it to do so first.
