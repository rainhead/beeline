# The upload to Ecdysis

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

`src/app/ecdysis-upload.ts` (beeline-8dx9.12) writes the two files a person uploads to Ecdysis by hand, one Ecdysis collection at a time, and only a collection Beeline reads whole (`ECDYSIS_COLLECTIONS`: British Columbia's collection 196 for the program BC; Washington's dataset 44 is not). The page is the last section of `/exports` (`src/app/views/exports.tsx`) and the files are served from `/exports/ecdysis/<program>-<year>-upload.zip` and `…-associations.csv`; staff administrators only, as the page is. The plan behind them is [publishing-to-ecdysis-and-gbif.md](../research/publishing-to-ecdysis-and-gbif.md), what Symbiota does with them is measured in [symbiota-import-test-2026-11-20.md](../research/symbiota-import-test-2026-11-20.md), and the two files Joel Gardner made by hand before are specified in [ecdysis-upload-files.md](../research/ecdysis-upload-files.md).

These are not the program archives (`src/app/dwc-archive.ts`, written nightly, never uploaded anywhere). They share that file's stream and its cell functions (`csvCellsOf` reads the staff download's cells by name; `specimenArchiveStream` takes an `ArchiveLayout`, which is the program archives' by default), and they have a column list of their own.

## Which collections, which specimens, and when

**Only a collection Beeline reads whole is offered files.** Beeline reads British Columbia as Ecdysis's collection 196 (`collectionId` in `ECDYSIS_COLLECTIONS`) and Washington as dataset 44, a subset of WSU's collection 164 (`datasetId`). A Symbiota upload adds no record to a dataset, and Symbiota matches a catalog number across the whole collection, so the day after an upload to Washington the same specimens would be offered again, and a record in the collection but outside the dataset would be replaced. The page shows Washington with a sentence saying so and the routes answer 409. When Beeline reads Washington by the collection (or by its catalog prefix, as the plan says), this rule lets it in without a change here.

A file for a collection and a collection year (the calendar year of a sample's first day, as the program archives count) holds the specimens that

1. belong to the collection's program, by the scope `programArchives` gives it;
2. have a label on paper (`specimen_on_paper`, through the listing's own `labels=printed`) and a label number of digits only (a label number that is a collector's name and a number is counted, and never shown);
3. have no `specimen_disposition` row whose disposition is anything but "in collection" (a missing pin, or a remark that old Beeline recorded the specimen twice, which may be a second record for one bee);
4. have no row in `specimen_catalog_number` (the listing's `ecdysis=not`), **and** whose would-be catalog number, the collection's prefix and the label number, is not in the archive Beeline last downloaded from Ecdysis, **and** whose label number is on no other specimen and whose catalog number fits Symbiota's 32 characters;
5. unless the downloader chooses otherwise, have no obscured coordinates, of two kinds counted apart: the taxon is obscured or private (`nullif(sample.taxon_geoprivacy, 'open') IS NOT NULL`), or the observer obscured or hid their own observation (`sample.geoprivacy`). Peter decided on 2026-10-23 that the judgment on these records is the taxonomist's, made before upload, so the page offers the choice for each kind, as `includeTaxonObscured=1` and `includeObserverObscured=1` on the file's address (two checkboxes in a form, with a button for each file), and says that including them puts their true coordinates in Ecdysis, where anyone can see them. A specimen in both kinds goes only when both are chosen. Everything else is written as a staff reader sees it, with true coordinates, as the program archives are. The choice is kept in the record of the download.

The fourth rule has two parts because the store keeps only the Ecdysis records the loader matched to a specimen: `specimen_catalog_number` holds those, and the loader counts the rest and keeps five as a sample (`unmatchedSample`). A record Ecdysis holds that Beeline could not match, say because its label number is on two specimens (beeline-1kb.14), would look new, and uploading its catalog number again would make Symbiota replace that record whole. So `catalogNumbersInArchive` reads every catalog number out of the cached archive file the nightly read keeps (`cachedArchivePath`, `data/ecdysis/collection-196.zip`), with DuckDB as the loader reads it, comparing without regard to case because Symbiota's column does (`utf8_general_ci`; confirmed on the test database, not on Ecdysis's). It unpacks the occurrences file into a folder of its own under `BEELINE_ECDYSIS`, which the app's store is allowed to read (`storeFiles`; `test/store-file-limit.test.ts` runs it under that limit), and removes it. Unpacks run one at a time, folders an earlier unpack left behind are removed first, two first views share one unpack, and the answer is kept in memory while the zip's size and time stay the same.

**Which years are offered.** Only a year in which Ecdysis holds no specimen of the program yet (a row in `specimen_catalog_number` for a specimen of the program that year) and which is an active season or the season that settled most recently, by the store's own season line (`season`, schema/102): from the year before the first active one to the current year. Peter decided that older years wait (GitHub #420 for Washington, beeline-2afe for British Columbia). Other years with specimens waiting are listed on the page as not offered, with their counts and a link to the Specimens listing for the program, the year and `ecdysis=not` (so the people who must decide can see them), and the routes refuse them with 409.

**Narrowing.** The Specimens listing's own filters in the query string narrow a file (`parseListingQuery`, `filteredSpecimens`). The listing's parser is forgiving on purpose, so `listingFilterProblems` names what it would have ignored: an unknown parameter, a repeated one, a value the parser does not read (a date that is no date, an unknown status or area). Any of those, or `scope`, `labels`, `ecdysis`, `year`, `sort`, `dir`, `page` and `act`, which are the rules or the file's own name and not choices, are answered 400 and nothing is written. A `qc` filter that reads the flag roll-up joins it (`needsRollup`); the earlier code left the join out for every `qc` value.

## When a file may be built

No file is written, and the page and the route say so in plain words, unless the last read of Ecdysis for the collection began under two hours ago. The reason is the same as for the archive being offered once: Symbiota replaces a record it matches whole, so a file made from an old read could replace a record someone added since, and a person must read Ecdysis again (the page has a button that posts to the existing `/jobs/run/ecdysis-fetch`) just before downloading.

The source is a marker the fetch writes beside the cached archive after every successful read, whether it downloaded or found the archive unchanged (`<collection>.read.json`, `ReadMarker` in `src/fetch-ecdysis.ts`): when the read began, which zip it loaded (size and modification time) and how many records. Earlier versions read the job's history and its prose; the marker is exact and says which zip, so a zip changed or replaced since the read is refused. The archive's own file time is not used, since it changes only when an archive is downloaded. A file is also refused when the marker is missing, the zip is missing or cannot be read, or the zip holds fewer distinct catalog numbers than `specimen_catalog_number` has with the collection's prefix (it is then not the whole of what Ecdysis held).

## One archive for each read

Table `ecdysis_upload_download` (`schema/177`, migration 0113) records each download of an archive: who, when, the collection, the year, the filters, the choice about obscured specimens, how many specimens, and the time of the read it rested on. An archive for a collection is refused while a download is recorded after the last read began, so there is one archive for each read of Ecdysis, whatever the year or the filters, and the page says who took it and when and to read Ecdysis again after uploading. The associations file for the same collection and year stays available: loading it twice adds nothing (it is rejected row by row as a duplicate; tested 2026-11-20).

The download is recorded when the stream completes (`recordWhenComplete`), which is when the server has written the whole file; the browser may still be receiving the last of it. A download the person abandons or that fails is not recorded: a partial zip is not a file that could be uploaded, and they may ask again. Two requests at once cannot both start (`claimDownload`: the second is told another archive is being written). The table has no foreign keys and is never updated (ADR 0007's shape: app-written events), and it was added by a migration because production is never reseeded (ADR 0006). The id of each archive row (the specimen's `entity_id`) and each identification's `sourceIdentifier` (the determination's `entity_id`) are entity ids and rest on production never being reseeded, which ADR 0006 states: a reseed renumbers them, and Symbiota would then update the wrong identifications in place.

## The rules hold when the file is written

The plan is made from the store at one moment and the file is written later, a page at a time. So each page of the archive asks again (`eligibleNow`, the same rules as the plan) which of its specimens may still go, and reads the specimens and their identifications in one transaction (`db.transaction()`, as `runCollectors` reads), so a page is one moment of the store: a specimen that has become obscured, been marked missing or held in Ecdysis since the plan is not written, and a determination added meanwhile is in its specimen's page or not at all. The associations file does the same. `pagedStream` was given an optional `last` on a page, since a page can be shorter than a full one without being the last. This is tested with a specimen that becomes obscured between planning and writing.

## The archive's columns

`occurrence.txt` and `meta.xml` declare each column under the term Symbiota maps. Every term was checked three ways: Symbiota's source (`SpecUploadDwca.php` 273-276 takes the last part of a term's address as the header; `SpecUploadBase.php` `echoFieldMapTable`, 344-440, lower-cases it, drops `_ . ( )` and spaces, applies a small translation map, and maps it where `uploadspectemp` has a column of that name), the importer's own mapping page (auto-mapped with no change by hand), and a live import (the stored value in the named column equals the archive's cell, for every cell of 31 records). Constants are real columns because whether Symbiota honours a `default` in `meta.xml` is untested, and they are each collection's own (`UPLOAD_CONSTANTS`): Washington's records carry `labelProject` "Washington Bee Atlas" (what Joel's files write; Symbiota's `labelProject` column maps by name, which was read from the source and not imported live), and British Columbia's file has no such column until BC says what its records carry. There is no `occurrenceID`: Washington's collection ignores an uploaded one (test B2). The `id` of each row is the specimen's `entity_id`; Symbiota keeps it in `dbpk` only for a record it inserts.

The last seven rows' cells (name, authorship, qualifier, determiner, date, sex, caste) are filled only where the determination of record is an expert's. Where it is a volunteer's, or there is none, they are empty and the record is created with no name, so that Ecdysis's own experts name it (Peter, 2026-11-20). The determination of record is the newest expert's, else the newest volunteer's (`determination_of_record`).

**The hand-made file's columns this file does not write.** Joel's Washington file has `verbatimElevation` (the same number as `minimumElevationInMeters`, so a duplicate), `verbatimEventDate` (the date reformatted as text; Symbiota keeps the original text of a date only when it must convert one, and Beeline's `eventDate` is already ISO), `georeferenceSource` or `georeferenceSources` ("label": Beeline's `location_source` is a different thing and must not be written there), `municipality` and `habitat` (Beeline holds neither: it has no town boundaries and no habitat, and Joel types them by hand; beeline-8dx9.2). The helper columns that feed the associations upload (`relationshipOfResource`, `associationType`, `relatedResourceID`, `relationshipRemarks`, `url`) belong to the associations file, not the archive.

## The identifications

`identification.txt` holds every determination of every specimen in the file, as history, with `https://symbiota.org/terms/identificationIsCurrent` 1 only on the determination of record when it is an expert's and 0 on every other, so a specimen with only volunteers' determinations has history and no current name (test A5). Its columns map as follows: `scientificName` to `sciname`; `scientificNameAuthorship`, `identificationQualifier`, `identifiedBy`, `dateIdentified` by name; `identificationIsCurrent` to `iscurrent` (translation map, SUB 389); and `sourceIdentifier`.

Symbiota's unique key on a determination is (record, `dateIdentified`, `identifiedBy`, `sciname`), and it keeps only the first of two rows that agree on all four, whatever their flags (test A6). `uploadIdentificationRows` therefore writes determinations of one specimen that have the same name, determiner and date (or lack of one) as one row, current if any of them is, with the current one's other cells; the comparison is `symbiotaKey`: without accents or case, with runs of white space as one, as Symbiota's columns compare. Two determinations that agree in everything but the qualifier or the sex are therefore written as one, and the later detail is lost in Ecdysis.

`sourceIdentifier` is the determination's own `entity_id`. Symbiota's importer maps the header `sourceIdentifier` to the column of that name (SUB 1405-1418): a determination already on the record with the same one is updated in place instead of added again, so loading the same file twice duplicates nothing. Darwin Core's own `identificationID` would be the right term, but the importer has no mapping for it. Patch 3.1 of Symbiota's schema marks the column "deprecated" and copies it to `instanceID`; this importer still reads and writes it, and a later Symbiota that dropped it would leave the header unmapped, which is harmless.

## The associations file

`associationsCsv` writes one row for each specimen in the file whose sample has an iNaturalist observation that still exists (`observationGone` is left out: no page to send anyone to), with exactly `subject identifier: catalogNumber`, `resourceUrl` (`https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/<id>`) and `verbatimSciname` (the host as the observation named it, at the rank Beeline holds, empty where there is none and where the sample's method names a trap, since the plant near a trap is not a host: the same test as `plantRelationship`): the headers Symbiota's "Import extended data" maps for an external link (`OccurrenceImport.php` 440-485). It has no `objectID`, so Symbiota's export shows the observation's address as the related resource, which GloBI recognises, and no `occurrenceID`, so the subject is found by catalog number alone, in one round of uploads. UTF-8, quoted by RFC 4180, with no byte order mark and no formula guard: the program's CSV downloads have both for spreadsheets, but this file is read by Symbiota, which loaded it the same with and without a mark, and a guard would write an apostrophe into a name that begins with `-`, `=`, `+` or `@`. A person loads it with the association type "Occurrence - External Link" and the relationship "hasHost", the only fitting choice the page offers today ("visits flowers of", which GloBI reads as a flower visit, is not on its list).

## What a person is told, and why

The page (`m.exports.ecdysis`) gives the steps in this order: read Ecdysis now (the button reuses the Jobs page's `POST /jobs/run/:name`; it redirects to Jobs, which shows the run); download both files and upload the archive once, today, then delete it, because uploading it again later would replace identifications made in Ecdysis since (Symbiota overwrites a matched record whole and never compares dates); the Ecdysis collection to upload into, named under each collection's heading; "Darwin Core archive" with "Match on Catalog Number" and "Import Identification History" switched on (the defaults make a second record for every catalog number); stop at the "Pending Data Transfer Report" unless it says "Records to be updated: 0" and the new records are the number in the file's name; the associations file on "Import extended data" with "Occurrence - External Link" and "hasHost"; read Ecdysis again, because the counts do not change and no second archive is offered until it has; localities are as the labels have them.

The file names carry the collection, the year, the number of specimens and the Pacific date and time written (`uploadFileName`), so an old one is recognised and thrown away and the number the pre-transfer report must show is in the name. Responses carry `cache-control: no-store`.

## Verified against a real Symbiota (2026-11-20)

A synthetic store of invented people and places (32 specimens in the file: an expert's determination of record, volunteers' only, none, two identical undated determinations, a trap sample with a range of dates, a host at genus and at family rank, no observation, a disposition and remark, quotes, semicolons, an accent) was imported through Symbiota's own upload pages into a live-managed collection that already held four other records. Every cell of every record landed in the column above. The four earlier records were byte-for-byte unchanged afterwards (hashed). Names and current flags were as specified, and the end date was in `eventDate2` with `endDayOfYear`. A second import of the same archive duplicated nothing and changed no value, but it moved every record's `dateLastModified`: Symbiota's update first blanks `countryCode` and `continent`, then fills them again, and the blanking is a change to the row. The 29 associations loaded through "Import extended data". Symbiota's own Darwin Core export then showed the record, its identifications and the association.

What the guards exist for, also measured: after an expert named a volunteer-only record in Symbiota's editor, importing the same archive again with matching on blanked the record's name, determiner and date and moved its `dateLastModified`, while the determination history kept the expert's current row, so the record and its history disagreed. Three identifications differing only by an accent, case or spacing in the determiner collapsed to the first row in the file, and the one flagged current was lost. "Import extended data" skips a catalog number the collection does not hold and shows no "create new records" box for associations; a request that carries that field by hand creates a blank record. Symbiota's "skeletal" upload type creates new records for catalog numbers the collection lacks, with the CSV's fields, `basisOfRecord` PreservedSpecimen and an "undetermined" determination by "unknown" dated "s.d.", the pattern seen on Washington's records. The pre-transfer report reads "Records to be updated: 0" and "New records: N" for a new-only archive, and "Records to be updated: N" followed by "Caution:   incoming records will replace existing records" for one that matches; the record count is unchanged until the button "Transfer Records to Central Specimen Table" is pressed. The scripts are not kept in the repository.
