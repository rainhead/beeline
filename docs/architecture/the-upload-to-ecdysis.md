# The upload to Ecdysis

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

`src/app/ecdysis-upload.ts` (beeline-8dx9.12) writes the two files a person uploads to Ecdysis by hand, one Ecdysis collection at a time, and only a collection Beeline reads whole (`ECDYSIS_COLLECTIONS`: British Columbia's collection 196 for the program BC; Washington's dataset 44 is not). The page is the last section of `/exports` (`src/app/views/exports.tsx`) and the archive is asked for with a `POST` to `/exports/ecdysis/<program>-<year>-upload.zip` (a form on the page; GET and HEAD answer 405) and the associations file is a `GET` of `/exports/ecdysis/downloads/<download id>/associations.csv`; staff administrators only, as the page is. The plan behind them is [publishing-to-ecdysis-and-gbif.md](../research/publishing-to-ecdysis-and-gbif.md), what Symbiota does with them is measured in [symbiota-import-test-2026-11-20.md](../research/symbiota-import-test-2026-11-20.md), and the two files Joel Gardner made by hand before are specified in [ecdysis-upload-files.md](../research/ecdysis-upload-files.md).

These are not the program archives (`src/app/dwc-archive.ts`, written nightly, never uploaded anywhere). They share that file's stream and its cell functions (`csvCellsOf` reads the staff download's cells by name; `specimenArchiveStream` takes an `ArchiveLayout`, which is the program archives' by default), and they have a column list of their own.

## Which collections, which specimens, and when

**Only a collection Beeline reads whole is offered files.** Beeline reads British Columbia as Ecdysis's collection 196 (`collectionId` in `ECDYSIS_COLLECTIONS`) and Washington as dataset 44, a subset of WSU's collection 164 (`datasetId`). A Symbiota upload adds no record to a dataset, and Symbiota matches a catalog number across the whole collection, so the day after an upload to Washington the same specimens would be offered again, and a record in the collection but outside the dataset would be replaced. The page shows Washington with a sentence saying so and the route answers 409. When Beeline reads Washington by the collection, the rule lets it in without a change here.

**A specimen is eligible on its own account.** A specimen is in an archive when it

1. belongs to the collection's program, by the scope `programArchives` gives it;
2. has a label on paper (`specimen_on_paper`, through the listing's own `labels=printed`) and a label number of digits only (a label number that is a collector's name and a number is counted, and never shown);
3. has no `specimen_disposition` row whose disposition is anything but "in collection" (a missing pin, or a remark that old Beeline recorded the specimen twice, which may be a second record for one bee);
4. has no row in `specimen_catalog_number` (the listing's `ecdysis=not`), **and** its would-be catalog number, the collection's prefix and the label number, is not in the archive Beeline last downloaded from Ecdysis, **and** its label number is on no other specimen and its catalog number fits Symbiota's 32 characters;
5. unless the downloader chooses otherwise, has no obscured coordinates, of two kinds counted apart: the taxon is obscured or private (`nullif(sample.taxon_geoprivacy, 'open') IS NOT NULL`), or the observer obscured or hid their own observation (`sample.geoprivacy`). Peter decided on 2026-10-23 that the judgment on these records is the taxonomist's, made before upload, so the downloader chooses for each kind (`includeTaxonObscured=1`, `includeObserverObscured=1`, two checkboxes in a form) and the page says that including them puts their true coordinates in Ecdysis, where anyone can see them. A specimen in both kinds goes only when both are chosen. The choice is kept in the record of the download. Everything else is written as a staff reader sees it, with true coordinates, as the program archives are;
6. is not one whose sample is in `sample_geoprivacy_stale` (schema/105: the sample's copy of its observation's flags disagrees with the observation's). Which flags are true is not known, so the downloader's choice cannot be applied to it; it is left out, counted as its own line, and never offered as a choice. It is eligible again at the next sync that corrects the copy.

Nothing about the specimen's year, or about what else of that year Ecdysis holds, makes it ineligible. After an upload and a new read, the same year therefore offers whatever is still eligible: what was left out earlier, and what was labelled later. That is intended.

**Which years a collection offers is a setting in code.** `UPLOAD_CONSTANTS[program].years`, beside the collection's other fixed values, lists them. British Columbia offers 2026 only, by Peter's decision of 2026-11-21: its older years wait on beeline-2afe, and Washington's on GitHub #420. Adding a year is therefore a code change, made when that is settled, so that no one uploads a year by accident. Years with eligible specimens that are not listed show on the page as not offered, with their counts and a link to the Specimens listing for the program, the year, `ecdysis=not` and `labels=printed`, which are the filters the counts use, and the route refuses them with 409. (The tests set the setting to the seasons their fixtures are dated in; one test pins what the code says.)

The fourth rule has two parts because the store keeps only the Ecdysis records the loader matched to a specimen: `specimen_catalog_number` holds those, and the loader counts the rest and keeps five as a sample (`unmatchedSample`). A record Ecdysis holds that Beeline could not match, say because its label number is on two specimens (beeline-1kb.14), would look new, and uploading its catalog number again would make Symbiota replace that record whole. So `catalogNumbersInArchive` reads every catalog number out of the cached archive file the nightly read keeps (`cachedArchivePath`, `data/ecdysis/collection-196.zip`), with DuckDB as the loader reads it, comparing without regard to case because Symbiota's column does (`utf8_general_ci`; confirmed on the test database, not on Ecdysis's). It unpacks the occurrences file into a folder of its own under `BEELINE_ECDYSIS`, which the app's store is allowed to read (`storeFiles`; `test/store-file-limit.test.ts` runs it under that limit), and removes it. Each unpack is chained, at the moment it is asked for, onto the one before it, so one cannot clear the leftover folders while another is using its own; leftovers from an unpack cut short are removed first; two first views share one unpack; and the answer is kept in memory while the zip's size and time stay the same.

**Narrowing.** The Specimens listing's own filters in the query string narrow an archive (`parseListingQuery`, `filteredSpecimens`). The listing's parser is forgiving on purpose, so `listingFilterProblems` names what it would have ignored: an unknown parameter, a repeated one, a value the parser does not read (a date that is no date, an unknown status or area). Any of those, a repeated `includeTaxonObscured` or `includeObserverObscured`, or `scope`, `labels`, `ecdysis`, `year`, `sort`, `dir`, `page` and `act`, which are the rules or the archive's own name and not choices, are answered 400 and nothing is written. A text filter (`q`, `place`, `collector`, `host`, `taxon`) made only of separators or of `%` and `_` is a 400 as well. The place filter reads `,,,` as no place and narrows nothing, and the listing's matching does not escape LIKE wildcards, so `q=%`, `collector=%` and `host=%` select everything; those are the holes found (`taxon` resolves a name first and selects nothing for an unknown one, `area` and `member` are keyed). The filters are recorded as a query string with its keys sorted (`URLSearchParams.toString()`), so a value containing an ampersand cannot read as two filters. A `qc` filter that reads the flag roll-up joins it (`needsRollup`); the first version left the join out for every `qc` value.

## When an archive may be built

No archive is written, and the page and the route say so in plain words, unless the last read of Ecdysis for the collection began under two hours ago. Symbiota replaces a record it matches whole, so an archive made from an old read could replace a record someone added since, and a person must read Ecdysis again (the page has a button that posts to the existing `/jobs/run/ecdysis-fetch`) just before downloading.

The source is a marker the fetch writes beside the cached archive after every successful read, whether it downloaded or found the archive unchanged (`<collection>.read.json`, `ReadMarker` in `src/fetch-ecdysis.ts`): when the read began, which zip it loaded (size and modification time) and how many occurrence rows the archive itself has. That count is made from the archive's own file, as the upload code counts it again from the zip. It is not the loader's `occurrences`, which counts a join to specimens and so counts an Ecdysis record twice when its label number is on two Beeline specimens (beeline-1kb.14): British Columbia's real zip has 9,452 occurrence rows against 9,438 specimens with a stored catalog number, so the figures do differ in production, and a comparison with the loader's count would refuse for good. The fetch also checks, when Ecdysis says nothing moved, that the kept zip is still the one the marker describes (size and time) before reloading it, and downloads again if not; a store with no marker downloads once. The upload code, for its part, compares the file's size and time with the marker's immediately before and after reading its catalog numbers, and refuses if either differs. The archive's own file time is not used, since it changes only when an archive is downloaded. An archive is refused when the marker is missing, the zip is missing or cannot be read, the zip is not the marker's (size, time), the zip has a different number of occurrence rows than the marker's record count, or a catalog number the read recorded in `specimen_catalog_number` is not in the zip. That last test uses `loaded_at`: the loader writes it with the read's own start time for every specimen matched at that read, so those catalog numbers must be in that read's zip. It does not compare with every catalog number in the store: `src/load-ecdysis.ts` never removes a `specimen_catalog_number` row (an occurrence missing from a later archive leaves its row as it was, by design, since a flat export need not be the whole collection), so a record Ecdysis has since deleted keeps a catalog number the zip lacks, and a check against all of them would refuse for good.

## One archive for each read

Three tables (`schema/177`, migration 0116), because rows are never updated here (ADR 0007). `ecdysis_upload_download` is the start of a download: who, when, the collection, the year, the filters, the choice about obscured specimens, the planned count and the time of the read it rested on. A download is an identity (the associations file is asked for by its id, and its specimens refer to it), so its key is an `entity_id` (ADR 0002; a log row nothing refers to would take a key of another name, as `event_change` has `change_id`). `ecdysis_upload_download_completed` is its end, a facet keyed by the download's id, with the number of specimens actually written; `ecdysis_upload_download_specimen` is the specimens the archive held, about 8,000 rows an upload. None has a foreign key, because DuckDB would then refuse to update a referenced row, and a key to `specimen` would stop specimens being edited.

An archive is refused for a collection when any download for it, started or finished, began after the last successful read began. So a second archive, whatever the year or the filters, needs a new read. A download without a completion that began under 15 minutes ago is shown as being written; an older one as unfinished; both block. An abandoned or failed download keeps its start and gets no completion, and still blocks until the next read: the person may hold a partial file, and a read is cheap. A zip that was cut short cannot be uploaded, so the cost of this is a read. A read that began before a download and finished after it also blocks, because the download began after the read's recorded start.

The decision and the start are written in one transaction (`startDownload`). DuckDB works on snapshots, so two requests that each see no download can both pass a check, and the check is not what keeps them apart. The table's `UNIQUE (collection, read_at)` is: DuckDB refuses a second row with the same key, so at most one archive can be started for a read. The loser's transaction is rolled back (its own rollback may fail too and hide the cause, so the loser asks again what is on record and answers "an archive was just started"). A test sends two requests together.

**The archive is built whole before anything is answered.** It is generated in memory (`buildArchive`: British Columbia's is about 145 KB, and a bound of 50 MB refuses a larger one with a clear error), the completion is then recorded with the specimens actually written, and only then is the archive sent, under a file name that carries the number actually written. So "completed" means "Beeline finished writing it", which is the boundary that matters, because the person may have the file from the moment it is sent; and the planned count can no longer differ from the name. A client that goes away after that leaves a completed download on the page, which is correct. A failure while building leaves the start and no completion, and it still blocks until the next read. While building, the read marker on disk is looked at before each page and at the end; if it is no longer the one the plan rested on (a newer read finished), the build stops, nothing is recorded as completed, and the answer says to start again (tested with the marker replaced mid-build). The archive is asked for with POST, as the print-run address download is, because asking records a start and uses up the read; the app's origin check covers it. HEAD is refused for the associations route too, since Hono runs the GET handler for HEAD and drops the body.

**Known gap.** Two requests that rest on two different reads finishing within the same instant have different `read_at` and so different keys, and both can start. Symbiota's report would then show the second upload as updates, which the instructions say to stop at.

**The page lists finished downloads whatever the state of the read** (`downloadViewsOf`): the most recent ten per collection, newest first, each with who, when, how many specimens and whether Ecdysis now holds them ("all in Ecdysis since the last read") or how many it does not hold yet, with its associations link; and any older finished download that still has specimens absent from Ecdysis, so the list is not cut before the outstanding ones are found. A specimen counts as in Ecdysis when it has a `specimen_catalog_number` row or, where the cached archive is in hand, its catalog number is in it. An unfinished download is listed while it blocks. The freshness check governs only starting a new archive. A sentence says an older archive must be deleted, not uploaded. A download is looked up by id in one query (`downloadById`).

The id of each archive row (the specimen's `entity_id`) and each identification's `sourceIdentifier` (the determination's `entity_id`) are entity ids and rest on production never being reseeded, which ADR 0006 states: a reseed renumbers them, and Symbiota would then update the wrong identifications in place. The same holds for the recorded specimens of a download.

## The associations file belongs to a download

The associations file is built from a finished download, by its id (`/exports/ecdysis/downloads/<id>/associations.csv`), for exactly the specimens that archive held (the third table), and takes no filters or choices of its own. It stays available after later reads for as long as the record does, because loading it twice adds nothing (Symbiota rejects each row as a duplicate; tested 2026-11-20). The page offers it beside each finished download, not beside the checkboxes. A download that did not finish has none. A trap sample has no row at all, decided by the sample's `kind` and not by the word "trap" in the free-text protocol: Symbiota applies one relationship, `hasHost`, to the whole file, and the plant near a trap is not a host. The archive's `associatedTaxa` uses the same test for "near" and "visits flowers of". Places that still decide by protocol text (`methodNamesTrap`, `methodNamesTrapSql`, `plantRelationship`): `src/legacy-export.ts`, `src/app/your-bees.ts`, `src/app/determine.ts`, the program archives' and downloads' `associatedTaxa` in `src/app/listings.ts`, and the two listing views and the record view.

## The rules hold when the archive is written

The plan is made from the store at one moment and the archive is written later, a page at a time. So each page asks again (`eligibleNow`, the same rules as the plan, and also the collection year and the listing filters the request gave: a sample whose date was corrected to another year since the plan belongs to another year's archive) which of its specimens may still go, and reads the specimens and their identifications in one transaction (`db.transaction()`, as `runCollectors` reads), so a page is one moment of the store: a specimen that has become obscured, been marked missing or held in Ecdysis since the plan is not written, and a determination added meanwhile is in its specimen's page or not at all. The specimens actually written are collected and recorded as the download's. `pagedStream` was given an optional `last` on a page, since a page can be shorter than a full one without being the last, and a fix: a page whose bytes the compressor held back wrote nothing, and a stream that returned without writing was never asked for the next page. The real archive is two pages of 5,000 specimens; the tests make the page size small.

## The archive's columns

`occurrence.txt` and `meta.xml` declare each column under the term Symbiota maps. Every term was checked three ways: Symbiota's source (`SpecUploadDwca.php` 273-276 takes the last part of a term's address as the header; `SpecUploadBase.php` `echoFieldMapTable`, 344-440, lower-cases it, drops `_ . ( )` and spaces, applies a small translation map, and maps it where `uploadspectemp` has a column of that name), the importer's own mapping page (auto-mapped with no change by hand), and a live import (the stored value in the named column equals the archive's cell, for every cell of 31 records). Constants are real columns because whether Symbiota honours a `default` in `meta.xml` is untested, and they are each collection's own (`UPLOAD_CONSTANTS`): Washington's records carry `labelProject` "Washington Bee Atlas" (what Joel's files write; Symbiota's `labelProject` column maps by name, which was read from the source and not imported live), and British Columbia's file has no such column until BC says what its records carry. There is no `occurrenceID`: Washington's collection ignores an uploaded one (test B2). The `id` of each row is the specimen's `entity_id`; Symbiota keeps it in `dbpk` only for a record it inserts.

The helper columns that feed the associations upload (`relationshipOfResource`, `associationType`, `relatedResourceID`, `relationshipRemarks`, `url`) belong to the associations file, not the archive.

## The identifications

`identification.txt` holds every determination of every specimen in the file, as history, with `https://symbiota.org/terms/identificationIsCurrent` 1 only on the determination of record when it is an expert's and 0 on every other, so a specimen with only volunteers' determinations has history and no current name (test A5). Its columns map as follows: `scientificName` to `sciname`; `scientificNameAuthorship`, `identificationQualifier`, `identifiedBy`, `dateIdentified` by name; `identificationIsCurrent` to `iscurrent` (translation map, SUB 389); and `sourceIdentifier`.

Symbiota's unique key on a determination is (record, `dateIdentified`, `identifiedBy`, `sciname`), and it keeps only the first of two rows that agree on all four, whatever their flags (test A6). `uploadIdentificationRows` therefore writes determinations of one specimen that have the same name, determiner and date (or lack of one) as one row, current if any of them is, with the current one's other cells; the comparison is `symbiotaKey`: without accents or case, with runs of white space as one, as Symbiota's columns compare. Two determinations that agree in everything but the qualifier or the sex are therefore written as one, and the later detail is lost in Ecdysis.

`sourceIdentifier` is the determination's own `entity_id`. Symbiota's importer maps the header `sourceIdentifier` to the column of that name (SUB 1405-1418): a determination already on the record with the same one is updated in place instead of added again, so loading the same file twice duplicates nothing. Darwin Core's own `identificationID` would be the right term, but the importer has no mapping for it. Patch 3.1 of Symbiota's schema marks the column "deprecated" and copies it to `instanceID`; this importer still reads and writes it, and a later Symbiota that dropped it would leave the header unmapped, which is harmless.

## The associations file

`associationsCsv` writes, for the specimens a finished download held, one row for each whose sample has an iNaturalist observation that still exists (`observationGone` is left out: no page to send anyone to) and is not a trap sample, with exactly `subject identifier: catalogNumber`, `resourceUrl` (`https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/<id>`) and `verbatimSciname` (the host as the observation named it, at the rank Beeline holds, empty where there is none): the headers Symbiota's "Import extended data" maps for an external link (`OccurrenceImport.php` 440-485). It has no `objectID`, so Symbiota's export shows the observation's address as the related resource, which GloBI recognises, and no `occurrenceID`, so the subject is found by catalog number alone, in one round of uploads. UTF-8, quoted by RFC 4180, with no byte order mark and no formula guard: the program's CSV downloads have both for spreadsheets, but this file is read by Symbiota, which loaded it the same with and without a mark, and a guard would write an apostrophe into a name that begins with `-`, `=`, `+` or `@`. A person loads it with the association type "Occurrence - External Link" and the relationship "hasHost", the only fitting choice the page offers today ("visits flowers of", which GloBI reads as a flower visit, is not on its list).

## What a person is told, and why

The page (`m.exports.ecdysis`) gives the steps in this order: read Ecdysis now (the button reuses the Jobs page's `POST /jobs/run/:name`; it redirects to Jobs, which shows the run); download the archive and upload it once, today, then delete it, because uploading it again later would replace identifications made in Ecdysis since (Symbiota overwrites a matched record whole and never compares dates), and the first archive of a year need not be the last, since what is left out is offered again after the next read; the Ecdysis collection to upload into, named under each collection's heading; "Darwin Core archive", making sure "Match on Catalog Number" (ticked by default, `specuploadmap.php` 449) and "Import Identification History" are ticked; stop at the "Pending Data Transfer Report" unless it says "Records to be updated: 0", the new records are the number in the archive's file name, and it shows no line "Records matching on catalog number that will be appended" (`specuploadprocessor.php` 168, from `SpecUploadBase.php` 815-846, with the warning "This will result in records with duplicate catalog numbers"; it appears when the switch is off); the associations file of that archive on "Import extended data" with "Occurrence - External Link" and "hasHost"; read Ecdysis again, because the counts do not change and no second archive is offered until it has; localities are as the labels have them.

The archive's file name carries the collection, the year, the number of specimens and the Pacific date and time written (`uploadFileName`), so an old one is recognised and thrown away and the number the pre-transfer report must show is in the name. Responses carry `cache-control: no-store`.

## Verified against a real Symbiota (2026-11-20)

A synthetic store of invented people and places (32 specimens in the file: an expert's determination of record, volunteers' only, none, two identical undated determinations, a trap sample with a range of dates, a host at genus and at family rank, no observation, a disposition and remark, quotes, semicolons, an accent) was imported through Symbiota's own upload pages into a live-managed collection that already held four other records. Every cell of every record landed in the column above. The four earlier records were byte-for-byte unchanged afterwards (hashed). Names and current flags were as specified, and the end date was in `eventDate2` with `endDayOfYear`. A second import of the same archive duplicated nothing and changed no value, but it moved every record's `dateLastModified`: Symbiota's update first blanks `countryCode` and `continent`, then fills them again, and the blanking is a change to the row. The 29 associations loaded through "Import extended data". Symbiota's own Darwin Core export then showed the record, its identifications and the association.

What the guards exist for, also measured: after an expert named a volunteer-only record in Symbiota's editor, importing the same archive again with matching on blanked the record's name, determiner and date and moved its `dateLastModified`, while the determination history kept the expert's current row, so the record and its history disagreed. Three identifications differing only by an accent, case or spacing in the determiner collapsed to the first row in the file, and the one flagged current was lost. "Import extended data" skips a catalog number the collection does not hold and shows no "create new records" box for associations; a request that carries that field by hand creates a blank record. Symbiota's "skeletal" upload type creates new records for catalog numbers the collection lacks, with the CSV's fields, `basisOfRecord` PreservedSpecimen and an "undetermined" determination by "unknown" dated "s.d.", the pattern seen on Washington's records. The pre-transfer report reads "Records to be updated: 0" and "New records: N" for a new-only archive, and "Records to be updated: N" followed by "Caution:   incoming records will replace existing records" for one that matches; the record count is unchanged until the button "Transfer Records to Central Specimen Table" is pressed. The scripts are not kept in the repository.
