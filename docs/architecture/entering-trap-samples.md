# Entering trap samples

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

Until November 2026 every sample came from iNaturalist or the old Beeline. Trap samples reached the old system only as spreadsheets staff prepared and uploaded, one row per specimen, a few batches a year of about a hundred specimens, while Lincoln Best kept Oregon's own registry of trap samples (1,608 of them, 2018 to 2025, about a hundred new each year) in a spreadsheet of his own. At that volume a form suits better than an upload (Peter, 2026-11-04), so `/trap-samples/new` (`src/trap-sample.ts`, `src/app/views/trap-sample.tsx`, beeline-3vs.2) is a form whose fields are the ones those spreadsheets carry that a label prints or a Darwin Core record would carry: the sample number, up to three collectors in order (named as staff's correction of a sample's collectors names them, `src/collector-match.ts`: a display name, folded to its letters and digits if need be, or a person's id or `inat:` login), the days the traps were set and collected, the kind of trap and how many, the specimen count, the point and its uncertainty, and the country, state, county and locality. Everything else in the spreadsheets (whether a bottle held bumble bees, the lab's processing times, cup colours) stays in them. A one-time import of the existing spreadsheets, as the old Beeline's records were imported, is to follow.

**Who may enter one**: staff, and anyone who is a collector on a trap sample already (Peter, 2026-11-04). It is derived rather than granted, so nobody has to be set up first; a grant for someone new to trapping is beeline-3vs.1. The check reads the effective person, so a delegate reaches the form for a trapper they act for, and the history credits whoever typed it. Editing a sample is narrower: staff, one of its collectors, or whoever entered it, as the correction form lets a collector edit only their own samples. **Once the sample's season settles** (`settled_sample`) the form closes to its collectors and its enterer and stays staff's (Peter, 2026-11-19). The place for it is `mayEditTrapSample`, which the form's routes, the sample page's Edit link and the locality write (`setPlace`) all ask; the samples listing carries `entered_settled` so its Place pencil and Edit link are not offered to them either (beeline-3vs.5).

**The store is the record** (beeline-ni5m.9), so the form writes the sample, its collectors, its point (as `staff_entry`) and its atlas (from the state, as minting assigns it) straight into the store, in one transaction on the print connection behind the print runs' lock, so that an edit cannot land between a freeze reading a sample and composing its labels. `sample_entry` (`schema/030`) marks the sample as entered here, with who entered it and when. That mark is what the rest of the app reads: the sample page and the samples listing's Edit link offer the trap form instead of the correction form, which writes to an old Beeline record and so cannot carry an entered sample, and the listing's Place pencil writes an entered sample's locality directly, any name, for the same people who may edit it (beeline-3vs.5); and the nightly writes the county from the point for an entered sample as for one with an observation ([Areas](areas.md)), so the county can be left empty on the form.

**The number** offered is the next in Oregon's series: the highest number after `OBAS-` on any sample, plus one, zero-padded to five digits, counting on across years (Peter, 2026-10-30). It is editable, for another series or a Washington trap code (`YPT3`), and a number another trap sample already has is refused. Only trap samples count (Peter, 2026-11-04): an `OBAS-` number belongs to the whole program, while a net sample's number belongs to its collector for the day, so thousands of net samples share "3". A Washington trap code reused in a later year is refused too, which is the guess most likely to need changing. **Effort** is written into `sampling_effort` as the old records wrote it, `6 vane traps`, from the trap count and the kind of trap; the protocol is the kind of trap as chosen or written in. **The specimen count** stays 0 until the bees are pinned and counted, so a bottle not yet worked through never waits to print.

**Editing** is the same form, at `/samples/:id/trap`, until the sample's labels are printed or frozen into a print run, since the run already holds their text. After that a correction is a staff matter: staff change its method and dates from the Method row's pencil on the sample page ([Editing and corrections](editing-and-corrections.md), beeline-0wbq).

**Creating them in bulk** is applying a samples file ([Loading an edited download](loading-an-edited-download.md#applying-the-new-rows-beeline-kgyd410)): the job calls `createTrapSample`, which takes an optional step to run in its transaction so the upload's record stands or falls with the sample.

## Deleting a trap sample

Staff may delete a trap sample **entered in Beeline** that nothing has been done with (beeline-kgyd.4.10, Peter, 2026-11-19), so that an upload applied by mistake can be undone: it has a `sample_entry` row, has never printed (`printed_sample`, which counts a specimen with no label as printed, as every imported one is), is in no live print run (`sample_label_in_progress`), and has no specimens. The sample page offers "Delete this sample" to staff who are not viewing as someone else, which opens `/samples/:id/delete`: a page that says what goes, or why nothing can, and a form, never a browser dialog. The delete (`deleteTrapSample`) runs in the sample's queue turn and behind the print runs' lock, and removes the sample and every row that names it. It finds those rows in the store (`duckdb_constraints()`, every table with a foreign key to `sample` other than `specimen`), so a table added later is deleted from too, and a test asserts none is left. It takes **two transactions**, the satellites and then the sample, because DuckDB will not delete a referenced row in the transaction that removed its references ([Printing](printing.md) says the same of cancelling a run); a crash between them leaves a sample with no collector, which `sample_primary_collector_invalid` names and deleting again finishes (below). **The sample change log cannot say that a sample is gone**: a sample that falls silent is recorded as nothing (ADR 0007), and every entry is a change to one of `SAMPLE_FIELDS`. So the deletion is recorded in `sample_deletion` (`schema/048`, an entity with its own key: the sample's id, its number, who and when), and said in the server's log; the sample's earlier history stays in the file under its id and the next full pass drops its snapshot row. The row is written in the first transaction, with the satellites' removal, and a sample that has a `sample_deletion` row, no entry mark and no collector is one whose deletion was cut short: `deletable` accepts it and deleting again finishes. The row stays after the sample is gone, so a deletion is visible. A fresh deletion always writes a new record, and finishing a cut-short one writes none, so an id drawn again after the store was put back to an earlier copy has a record for each sample that had it, and an old record cannot make a live sample deletable (finishing also requires no entry mark and no collector). `upload_sample_outcome` rows are left alone: they have no foreign key to `sample`, so the record that an upload made it stands.

Not done yet: holds and waivers on an entered sample (they are keyed by an observation until beeline-9cfx), pooled samples made from other bottles, and the sites of beeline-3vs, which are set aside.
