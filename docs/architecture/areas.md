# Areas

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#architecture-overview).

An area is a named place with an outline that a sample's true coordinates can lie inside (beeline-9jd4). There are two kinds so far: the federal wilderness, because collecting there needs a permit the program usually lacks (beeline-zdfz), and the county, below. A sample inside a wilderness says so on its sample page and its specimens' pages, under the place, and the listings' place filter finds it by the wilderness's name, so "wilderness" finds every sample inside one. **It is a note, not a flag** (Peter, 2026-10-26): the specimens exist either way, so nothing blocks their labels. Beeline does not track permits yet, so the note says that collecting there needs one, not whether the program had one; inside Olympic National Park it did. Wilderness study areas do not count.

**The source is Wilderness.net's national layer** (Wilderness Connect), every agency's wildernesses, keyed by its `WID`, which is the id in Wilderness.net's own URLs and survives a rename: 428 is the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness, the Olympic Wilderness until 2017 (Peter, 2026-10-26). OpenStreetMap's outlines, which the locality step already had, missed 18% of the samples it finds on the sandbox store: Potrillo Mountains (designated in 2019), Colorado's (the gazetteer has no Colorado extract), and the edges of the Evans and Steens wildernesses. PAD-US remains the plan for the other US kinds; its live service drops wilderness altogether, and its download is a geodatabase DuckDB cannot read. `scripts/fetch-gazetteer.sh` fetches the layer in three pages, `ingest/gazetteer.sql` makes one row per wilderness of it (a wilderness two agencies manage arrives as two features with one `WID`), and the gazetteer carries it like every other source ([Localities from coordinates](localities-from-coordinates.md)).

**In the store** (`schema/038`), `area` is reference data keyed by `(source, source_id)`, not an entity (ADR 0002): an `entity_id` is drawn afresh by every reseed, and a page addressed by one would move. Only the areas a sample lies inside are copied in: on the sandbox store, 41 of the gazetteer's 806 wildernesses and 210 of its 3,264 counties and districts. Nothing records that one area lies inside another, a park's county or a county's state (Peter, 2026-10-26): only a sample is inside an area, and anything else is to be worked out from the outlines when a page asks. `geom` is `GEOMETRY`, a core type in DuckDB 1.5, so the store opens, copies, backs up and migrates without the spatial extension; only the derive step loads it. `sample_area` has no foreign key to `area`, because DuckDB updates a referenced row as a delete and an insert and refuses the delete, which would stop a new gazetteer from refreshing an area's outline.

**The derive step** (`src/derive-area.ts`) runs in the nightly after elevations and before localities, in "Check iNaturalist now" for the checked person's samples, and by hand as `pnpm area:derive`. It works as the locality step does: a sample is tested when it has not been, when its point has moved, or when its test was against another gazetteer build, in batches that are each a transaction, and it never fails the run. `sample_area_derivation` records the point and build each sample was tested at, which is what tells a sample inside nothing from one not yet tested, and `sample_area_current` (`schema/173`) is what the pages read: areas found for a point the sample no longer has are not shown. An area no sample lies inside any more is removed. The whole sandbox store takes 5 seconds.

**Inside means more than 100 m inside.** The program allows 100 m for GPS error (Peter, 2026-10-26), so a point counts as inside a wilderness only when it is more than 100 m from the outline's nearest edge (`WILDERNESS_EDGE_M`), the edges of a cut-out road corridor included. Steens Mountain is why: its outline comes in 14 parts with the roads between them, and most samples inside it were a few dozen metres from an edge, taken beside the Loop Road. On the sandbox store, 855 samples lie inside a wilderness's outline and 555 more than 100 m inside: 79 from the active seasons and 476 settled, of which the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness, inside Olympic National Park where the program has a permit, holds 48 and 67. Steens falls from 179 to 72. The point's own coordinate uncertainty is not used: most samples report none, or one smaller than the error the allowance covers.

**Withheld where the location is.** An area's name says where the point is, so the note and the place filter both go through the location rule (`withheld`, [Browsing](browsing-scope-filters-sort.md)): a reader who may not see where a sample is neither sees the note nor finds the sample by its wilderness.

**Counties.** A US county or a BC regional district is a kind of area too (`county`), so a sample's county can be read from its point and the boundaries in force now, rather than from iNaturalist, which still carries BC districts abolished years ago (beeline-313x). The sources are the Census Bureau's cartographic county boundaries, keyed by GEOID (41003 is Benton County, Oregon), and BC's legally defined administrative areas, keyed by the province's `LGL_ADMIN_AREA_ID`. A county is simply the one the point is in, with no allowance at the line. It is not shown on any page as an area, but the place filter finds a sample by its county's full name ("Comox Valley Regional District").

**Beeline writes the county itself** (Peter, 2026-10-27, beeline-313x). After finding the areas, the nightly writes each sample's `county` from the county its point is in, as the gazetteer writes it: the Census's name in the US (Benton, Doña Ana), and in BC the district's name as iNaturalist writes it (Capital, Comox Valley), which a label prints as the province abbreviates it, CAPRD and CMXRD (`src/label-text.ts`). A name and not the abbreviation, so screens stay readable and the legacy-format export never uses CRD for both Capital, as the old system did, and Cariboo, as the province does (Peter, 2026-10-27). Only on a sample that has an observation, is from an active season, has no label on paper, is not frozen into a print run that has not printed, has a state or province matching its point's, and lies in exactly one county. A frozen sample's run already holds its label text, so its record keeps the county the labels will carry (Peter, 2026-10-27). A printed sample's county is what its labels say, as a printed locality is. A settled sample with no print recorded was most likely printed before Beeline kept the record and will not print again (Peter, 2026-10-27). Samples without an observation are the old system's records, whose county the record or a staff correction states. Minting still fills an empty county from iNaturalist, and the next nightly replaces it. A point on the shore just outside every county, which the cartographic boundaries leave out because they stop at the shoreline, takes the nearest county within 1 km (`COUNTY_SHORE_M`). The nightly records what it changed in the sample log in a pass of its own, credited to "its coordinates", so the next promotion's pass does not credit it to an iNaturalist sync; `pnpm area:derive` does the same against the store whose log it keeps. "Check iNaturalist now" finds the areas but leaves the county to the nightly, because it records no sample changes. On the sandbox store the first stamp changed one county (Stikine to Stikine Region): iNaturalist already writes most district names this way, and the three still carrying Greater Vancouver, renamed Metro Vancouver in 2017, are frozen into a run not yet printed.

The gazetteer's region ids, which the locality rule reads, are built from the GEOID in the US (US-OR-41003) and from the province's abbreviation in BC (CA-BC-CAPRD), not from names, because US county names collide: Virginia has a Richmond County and a Richmond city, and six such pairs shared an id, all in states no atlas covers. The locality rule reads a region id only to ask "same county?" and "same state?", so no locality in an atlas changed.

Measured before Beeline wrote counties itself. So in the US the point and the stored county agree for current work, and the settled disagreements are older records with the wrong county (Klamath where the point is in Jackson, 73). In BC more than a quarter of the samples carry a spelling the province does not use, and 541 a district that no longer exists: Comox-Strathcona was split in 2008, and of the 500 samples still marked CxSRD, 385 lie in Comox Valley and 115 in Strathcona. 202 located samples are in no county. 136 are on the Washington and Oregon shore, a median 13 m and 56 m outside the cartographic boundaries, which stop at the shoreline; the rest are in provinces and countries the gazetteer does not cover. Those on the shore now take the nearest county within 1 km, below.

**Never a locality.** The locality step still leaves out every OpenStreetMap park named "… Wilderness". Judging a park by how much of it lies inside a Wilderness.net outline was measured and rejected: Olympic, North Cascades and Mount Rainier National Parks are 93–96% wilderness and would lose their names.
