# Determinations from published archives

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

`pnpm archive:fetch <gbif-dataset-key> [--version v]` then `pnpm archive:load <archive-dir> [db] [--dry-run]` (`src/fetch-archive.ts`, `src/load-archive.ts`, beeline-muke) read the Darwin Core archive a museum publishes to GBIF, and keep what it says about Beeline's specimens. The first dataset is OSAC's "Oregon Bee Atlas" (GBIF dataset `1fa69ff3-3e40-465f-927f-12b1484d3731`), the only place Oregon's expert identifications are published: OSAC's own database is private, and since cutover the old Beeline sends back only volunteers' identifications. The tables are in `schema/042`, the views in `schema/112`.

**What a load records, and what it only shows** (Peter, 2026-11-15: "play this conservatively, in the hopes we will get this data directly from Linc instead"). The archive gives one current identification per record, with a bare year and no time of change, and the old Beeline's expert names carry no date at all. So where Beeline already holds an expert's name for a specimen, nothing says whether the archive's is newer. A load therefore records a determination in two cases only:

- the specimen has no expert determination: the archive's name is recorded as one and becomes the record;
- the specimen's determination of record is the one an earlier load recorded from the same record, and the record now says something else: the publisher revised its own statement and nobody has spoken in Beeline since.

Everything else is kept as the archive's statement and shown on the specimen's page, under its determinations, as what the museum publishes (`archive_note`), whether it agrees with Beeline's name or not. A statement that differs from the expert determination of record is also listed in `archive_disagreement`, with the season split every check has. Nothing Beeline holds is changed in place, and nothing the archive says is hidden (Peter, the same day: every change relative to the old Beeline, and from now on, should be visible). The test is where Beeline's name came from, not whether the names match: an expert who confirms the archive's name in Beeline has spoken since, so a later revision by the publisher does not replace it.

Measured on production's backup of 15 November 2026 with version 1.9 of OSAC's dataset (64,927 records, all collected 2017 to 2024, so all in settled seasons): 17,274 specimens had no expert name and would get one, 17,264 of them identified by Lincoln Best in 2024 and 17,273 of them to species; 40,600 specimens' expert name is the one the archive gives; 6,589 differ, and in 5,876 of those the archive gives only the genus where Beeline has a species in it, each with a year of 2019 or 2020. The load takes about two seconds.

**The tables.** `archive_dataset` names a dataset by GBIF's key and says which column of its archive is the specimen's field number; a load refuses a dataset that is not there. `archive_version` is each version loaded, in the order loaded, which is the order published: a version number does not sort as text, and OSAC published two versions on one day. `archive_statement` is what the archive said about a record, as published, from the version that first said it; a version that repeats a statement adds no row, one that changes it adds one, and a record a version drops gets a row marked withdrawn. Statements are never updated. `archive_reading` is what the last load made of each record's newest statement (which specimen, which name in the tree, or why not) and is replaced for the whole dataset by every load, so a name the tree has since gained or a corrected field number is read afresh. `archive_determination` says which record and version each recorded determination came from.

**Their channel is `worksheet_import`**, with `is_expert` true, because DuckDB cannot add a channel value to a deployed store's CHECK (ADR 0006), as with an expert's sheet ([Files people send](files-people-send-and-expert-sheets.md)). A row in `archive_determination`, and none in `worksheet_determination`, is what says a determination came from an archive. The specimen page labels it from that row ("from the dataset OSAC publishes, version 1.9") and the API's `determinations` carry `archive_publisher` and `archive_version`. The volunteer worksheet loader ignores expert rows. The Your bees page leaves these out of the specimens it reads for dating, since `expert_identification` (`schema/111`) dates a stated day and nothing coarser: a determination from an archive is undated, so a load announces nothing on the front page. The legacy-format comparison files a difference on such a specimen as `newer_determination`, which it is.

**A determination is recorded as the archive states it and no further**: the name, with `scientificName` as `verbatim_identification`; the sex; the determiner through `ingest/determiner-aliases.csv`; the year, kept as a year. OSAC's archive has no caste and none is supplied. An independent review of the plan found that copying sex or caste from an earlier determination would write rows saying a determiner stated what they did not.

**The record is matched to a specimen by its field number, whatever its atlas** (181 of OSAC's records are specimens Beeline files under Idaho or Washington), and the number is confirmed as a typed number in a sheet is: one of the record's collectors must be one of the sample's, by whole name or family name, and the day collected must fall within the sample's. A record whose number is in doubt is tied to no specimen and shown on no page: a number no specimen carries (393 on that backup), a number two records give (OSAC publishes one specimen twice, under its catalog number and under its field number), a collector or a day that disagrees (69), and a record an earlier load recorded on another specimen, which happens when a field number is corrected between loads and is for a person to sort out. A name the tree lacks is adopted from Ecdysis's thesaurus only for a record that would be recorded (`src/adopt-ecdysis.ts`); for one that is only shown, the tree is left alone, and the page shows the name as published. Held records are written to `held.csv` in the archive's directory, and whoever runs the load files a bead for those that kept a determination from being recorded, as for a sheet.

**The archive is the publisher's, not GBIF's.** GBIF's registry says where the publisher's archive is, and that is the file read. GBIF's copy of the same records respells names to its own taxonomy (*Calliopsis anthidius* becomes *anthidia*) and replaces some years with a timestamp, and its download needs an account. The `occurrenceID` is kept exactly as published, scheme included: OSAC writes `http:` on about 11,000 and `https:` on the rest, and it never changed one across the six versions it published in 2026. OSAC's IPT keeps earlier versions at the same address (`--version 1.6`), though it answers 401 for versions 1.1 to 1.4. The version recorded is the one the archive's own `eml.xml` states in `packageId`.

**Loading again.** The same version again stores no statements and records nothing new, but reads every statement again, so a record held for a spelling is recorded once the alias exists. The same version number over a different file is refused, by the hash of the occurrence file. A version published before the newest loaded is refused. Both commands are run by hand: OSAC changed 16 names across its six versions of 2026, so there is no job, and a job would need a page for held records first.

**Not done, on purpose.** Where the archive agrees with Beeline's name, its year and determiner are shown but not recorded, so Oregon's expert names from the old Beeline stay undated. Recording them as new determinations needs the pages and the Darwin Core export to read sex and caste from the newest expert determination that states one, as the legacy-format export already does; otherwise a determination with no caste would hide the caste beneath it (16,635 of those specimens have one). OSAC's catalog numbers and occurrenceIDs are kept in the statements and not yet written to the exports (beeline-1kb.22). The legacy-format export's subgenus column gives the tree's subgenus for a specimen named this way, not the one the archive states.
