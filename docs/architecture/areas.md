# Areas

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is [README.md](README.md).

An area is a named place with an outline that a sample's true coordinates can lie inside (beeline-9jd4). The kinds are the federal wilderness, because collecting there needs a permit the program usually lacks (beeline-zdfz); the county and the ecoregion, below; and the places people collect in and name, which are refuges, wildlife area units, parks and islands ([below](#the-places-people-collect-in)). A sample inside a wilderness says so on its sample page and its specimens' pages, under the place, and the listings' place filter finds it by the wilderness's name (and by a county's, an ecoregion's, a refuge's, a park's or an island's: it reads every area's name, whatever its kind), so "wilderness" finds every sample inside one. **It is a note, not a flag** (Peter, 2026-10-26): the specimens exist either way, so nothing blocks their labels. Beeline does not track permits yet, so the note says that collecting there needs one, not whether the program had one; inside Olympic National Park it did. Wilderness study areas do not count.

**The source is Wilderness.net's national layer** (Wilderness Connect), every agency's wildernesses, keyed by its `WID`, which is the id in Wilderness.net's own URLs and survives a rename: 428 is the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness, the Olympic Wilderness until 2017 (Peter, 2026-10-26). OpenStreetMap's outlines, which the locality step already had, missed 18% of the samples it finds on the sandbox store: Potrillo Mountains (designated in 2019), Colorado's (the gazetteer has no Colorado extract), and the edges of the Evans and Steens wildernesses. PAD-US remains the plan for the other US kinds; its live service drops wilderness altogether, and its download is a geodatabase DuckDB cannot read. `scripts/fetch-gazetteer.sh` fetches the layer in three pages, `ingest/gazetteer.sql` makes one row per wilderness of it (a wilderness two agencies manage arrives as two features with one `WID`), and the gazetteer carries it like every other source ([Localities from coordinates](localities-from-coordinates.md)).

**In the store** (`schema/038`), `area` is reference data keyed by `(source, source_id)`, not an entity (ADR 0002): an `entity_id` is drawn afresh by every reseed, and a page addressed by one would move. Only the areas a sample lies inside are copied in: on the sandbox store, 41 of the gazetteer's 806 wildernesses and 210 of its 3,264 counties and districts. Nothing records that one area lies inside another, a park's county or a county's state (Peter, 2026-10-26): only a sample is inside an area, and anything else is to be worked out from the outlines when a page asks. `geom` is `GEOMETRY`, a core type in DuckDB 1.5, so the store opens, copies, backs up and migrates without the spatial extension; only the derive step loads it. `sample_area` has no foreign key to `area`, because DuckDB updates a referenced row as a delete and an insert and refuses the delete, which would stop a new gazetteer from refreshing an area's outline.

**The derive step** (`src/derive-area.ts`) runs in the nightly after elevations and before localities, in "Check iNaturalist now" for the checked person's samples, and by hand as `pnpm area:derive`. It works as the locality step does: a sample is tested when it has not been, when its point has moved, or when its test was against another gazetteer build, in batches that are each a transaction, and it never fails the run. `sample_area_derivation` records the point and build each sample was tested at, which is what tells a sample inside nothing from one not yet tested, and `sample_area_current` (`schema/173`) is what the pages read: areas found for a point the sample no longer has are not shown. An area no sample lies inside any more is removed. The whole sandbox store takes 5 seconds.

**Inside means more than 100 m inside.** The program allows 100 m for GPS error (Peter, 2026-10-26), so a point counts as inside a wilderness only when it is more than 100 m from the outline's nearest edge (`WILDERNESS_EDGE_M`), the edges of a cut-out road corridor included. Steens Mountain is why: its outline comes in 14 parts with the roads between them, and most samples inside it were a few dozen metres from an edge, taken beside the Loop Road. On the sandbox store, 855 samples lie inside a wilderness's outline and 555 more than 100 m inside: 79 from the active seasons and 476 settled, of which the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness, inside Olympic National Park where the program has a permit, holds 48 and 67. Steens falls from 179 to 72. The point's own coordinate uncertainty is not used: most samples report none, or one smaller than the error the allowance covers.

**Withheld where the location is.** An area's name says where the point is, so the note and the place filter both go through the location rule (`withheld`, [Browsing](browsing-scope-filters-sort.md)): a reader who may not see where a sample is neither sees the note nor finds the sample by its wilderness.

**Counties.** A US county or a BC regional district is a kind of area too (`county`), so a sample's county can be read from its point and the boundaries in force now, rather than from iNaturalist, which still carries BC districts abolished years ago (beeline-313x). The sources are the Census Bureau's cartographic county boundaries, keyed by GEOID (41003 is Benton County, Oregon), and BC's legally defined administrative areas, keyed by the province's `LGL_ADMIN_AREA_ID`. A county is simply the one the point is in, with no allowance at the line. It is not shown on any page as an area, but the place filter finds a sample by its county's full name ("Comox Valley Regional District").

**Beeline writes the county itself** (Peter, 2026-10-27, beeline-313x). After finding the areas, the nightly writes each sample's `county` from the county its point is in, as the gazetteer writes it: the Census's name in the US (Benton, Doña Ana), and in BC the district's name as iNaturalist writes it (Capital, Comox Valley), which a label prints as the province abbreviates it, CAPRD and CMXRD (`src/label-text.ts`). A name and not the abbreviation, so screens stay readable and the legacy-format export never uses CRD for both Capital, as the old system did, and Cariboo, as the province does (Peter, 2026-10-27). Only on a sample that has an observation or was entered in Beeline ([Entering trap samples](entering-trap-samples.md)), is from an active season or a settled one staff released for printing ([Printing](printing.md)), has no label on paper, is not frozen into a print run that has not printed, has a state or province matching its point's, and lies in exactly one county. A frozen sample's run already holds its label text, so its record keeps the county the labels will carry (Peter, 2026-10-27). A printed sample's county is what its labels say, as a printed locality is. A settled sample that staff have not released, with no print recorded, was most likely printed before Beeline kept the record and will not print again (Peter, 2026-10-27). Other samples without an observation are the old system's records, whose county the record or a staff correction states. Minting still fills an empty county from iNaturalist, and the next nightly replaces it. A point on the shore just outside every county, which the cartographic boundaries leave out because they stop at the shoreline, takes the nearest county within 1 km (`COUNTY_SHORE_M`). The nightly records what it changed in the sample log in a pass of its own, credited to "its coordinates", so the next promotion's pass does not credit it to an iNaturalist sync; `pnpm area:derive` does the same against the store whose log it keeps. "Check iNaturalist now" finds the areas but leaves the county to the nightly, because it records no sample changes. On the sandbox store the first stamp changed one county (Stikine to Stikine Region): iNaturalist already writes most district names this way, and the three still carrying Greater Vancouver, renamed Metro Vancouver in 2017, are frozen into a run not yet printed.

**The state and country follow the point too** (beeline-1kb.17.1, GitHub #298), where the point lies inside the county's outline. A county taken by the shore allowance is written only where its state is already the sample's, since 500 m into Alberta the nearest district is in BC. **A point staff set** (`sample_location_override`) is followed the same way on any sample, printed or settled. A person said that is where the bees were caught, so a place that disagrees is the record contradicting itself, and on a printed sample the labels already disagree with the point and a reprint is what comes next. Sample 797063 is why: it printed in Nevada County, California, because its collector's pin was on the wrong Wolf Mountain. She moved it to Lane County, Oregon, and nothing could change the state and county to match. The atlas moves with the state where geography had assigned it; a person's assignment stays. Following the state on samples still to print is also what lets a staff point be taken away: before it, the state the staff point brought would have stayed behind (Fable's review). Only a run that has not printed holds a sample back, since its labels were composed at the freeze. The staff coordinates routes run the step for their one sample at once (`stampSampleIds`), with the elevation, before logging the change, so the page that comes back is whole and the log credits the staffer. They refuse while the nightly or the weekly sweep runs, and where the step cannot settle the place (no gazetteer, a point in no county or on the shore) the page tells staff to check the county and state before reprinting. On the store of 2026-11-10 the new rule changed no sample in either season: every one still to print already agreed with its point. Which samples follow their point is said once, as the view `sample_follows_point` (`schema/150`, read through `followsPointSql`), which the held-out wait of a released settled sample (`sample_awaiting_place`) reads as well, so that nothing the stamp would write prints before the stamp has looked at it, and which the staff correction of a sample's country, state and county reads too: it refuses those samples, since the stamp would write over it by morning, unless the areas step found the point in no county or in two ([Editing and corrections](editing-and-corrections.md), beeline-kgyd.4.3). Every other sample keeps the place its record or a staff correction states.

**What the point says the state is, recorded** (beeline-rax1). The areas step records, beside the point and build in `sample_area_derivation`, the country and state or province of the sample's one county; `county_inside`, which says whether the point lies inside that county or only took it by the shore allowance; and `county_inside_100m`, whether the point is at least 100 m inside that county. It is a statement about the sample's point, not an attribute of the county, so it keeps to the ruling that nothing records one area inside another. It is also a different fact from `sample.state_province`, which is what the record and its labels say: the stamp writes the one from the other where a sample follows its point, and reads "inside" and the point's state from this row rather than testing the outlines itself. That removed the stamp's own spatial join (the one that ran out of memory in beeline-6cur) and its second definition of inside: a point exactly on a county line, which `ST_Contains` gives to no county, used to count as inside under the stamp's `ST_Intersects`, and now does not. Whether a point is 100 m inside is asked as whether a square 200 m across, centred on it, lies wholly inside the outline, which a spatial join answers reading each outline in place. The distance to the edge would have been the plainer answer, but it builds the outline's boundary afresh on every sample's row: 5,000 points in the Stikine Region, the largest outline, ran out of 512 MB that way (CodeRabbit), where the square fits in 128 MB. Its corners reach about 141 m, so a point a little more than 100 m from the edge may count as near it. `AREA_RULE_VERSION` in `src/derive-area.ts` is on every row, as the locality rule's version is, so raising it tests every sample again; migration 0101 brought the existing rows across as version 0, which the next nightly tests again. On production that is every sample, as it was on 7 November when the gazetteer with ecoregions went up, on the same machine.

**Where the record and the point disagree** (beeline-rax1). `sample_place_disagreement` (`schema/173`) lists the samples whose state or country is not what their point says: mostly those the stamp leaves alone on purpose (printed, settled, old-system records, frozen into a run). It carries `printed` and `settled`, so a count is read by season. A point within 100 m of its county's edge is left out, the program's allowance for GPS error as for wildernesses. On production on 2026-11-17, 32 samples disagreed, and ten of them were within 100 m by this test (one of them 123 m from the edge, under a corner), two of the three active-season ones among them: samples 12 and 21 m inside Montana on the Idaho line, recorded ID. The third stays in, and is wrong the other way: a point 33 m into Yukon, correctly recorded YT, which the BC outline, simplified to about 30 m, reaches over by more than 100 m. What remains is that sample and 21 settled ones, most of them far from any line (Oregon records with points in Idaho, Washington, or near Victoria, BC, kilometres from the border). A point that took the nearest county on the shore stays in, because near a land border that county can be across it, and the page says so in those words. A sample in no county or in two is never listed: its point says nothing about the state. On that day 67 located samples had no county, 1 of them active. It is a report and not a QC finding (Peter, 2026-11-17): it blocks nothing, and on a printed or settled sample the volunteer can rarely fix it. Staff see it on the sample page ("These coordinates are in NV, USA, but this record says OR, USA"), since only staff correct a printed place, and decide whether the pin or the labels are wrong. The API's sample carries it too, withheld wherever the location is, since a reader who may see the point may see which state it is in.

The gazetteer's region ids, which the locality rule reads, are built from the GEOID in the US (US-OR-41003) and from the province's abbreviation in BC (CA-BC-CAPRD), not from names, because US county names collide: Virginia has a Richmond County and a Richmond city, and six such pairs shared an id, all in states no atlas covers. The locality rule reads a region id only to ask "same county?" and "same state?", so no locality in an atlas changed.

So in the US the point and the stored county agree for current work, and the settled disagreements are older records with the wrong county (Klamath where the point is in Jackson, 73). In BC more than a quarter of the samples carry a spelling the province does not use, and 541 a district that no longer exists: Comox-Strathcona was split in 2008, and of the 500 samples still marked CxSRD, 385 lie in Comox Valley and 115 in Strathcona. 202 located samples are in no county. 136 are on the Washington and Oregon shore, a median 13 m and 56 m outside the cartographic boundaries, which stop at the shoreline; the rest are in provinces and countries the gazetteer does not cover. Those on the shore now take the nearest county within 1 km, below.

**Ecoregions** (beeline-l5so). A sample's EPA Level III ecoregion is read from its point, so the stewardship reports (GitHub #282), the rareness analysis (#284) and the communiqué's sampling-completeness analysis by Oregon ecoregion (beeline-2zej.3) join one answer rather than each carrying a GeoPackage. The source is the EPA's North American Level III file (`NA_CEC_Eco_Level3`, from the Commission for Environmental Cooperation), keyed by its `NA_L3CODE` (7.1.9 is the Willamette Valley; Corvallis is in it). The file cuts each ecoregion into many patches, so the gazetteer dissolves them to one row per ecoregion (`ecoregion`, `ingest/gazetteer.sql`), 181 of them; code 0.0.0, the Great Lakes' "Water", is left out. The file comes at full resolution, 3.1 million vertices, as many as all 806 wildernesses, while the other layers arrive simplified to about 30 m, so each dissolved outline is simplified to the same tolerance (`ST_SimplifyPreserveTopology`, 0.0003°), which keeps a third of the vertices. Simplifying neighbours one by one can open a sliver between them or lap one over the other; on 300,000 random points over the four atlases' states (2026-11-06) the full and the simplified outlines agreed on every point but one on the shore, and none fell in two. The derive step keeps one ecoregion per sample regardless, the lower code, since the pages and the counts assume one. **Level III, not Level IV**: beeatlas.net loads the same file for its `ecoregion_l3` column, and old Beeline's stewardship-report GeoPackage is the same Level III set with 181 names, ten of them touching Oregon (Willamette Valley, Coast Range, Cascades, Klamath Mountains, Blue Mountains, Columbia Plateau, Eastern Cascades Slopes and Foothills, Northern Basin and Range, Snake River Plain, Strait of Georgia/Puget Lowland), so the reports and the column agree with theirs. Level IV waits for a consumer. An ecoregion is simply the one the point is in, with no allowance at the line; the ecoregions tile the land and stop at the shoreline, so a point on a beach takes the nearest within 1 km (`ECOREGION_SHORE_M`, the county's allowance), as one row, and the derive step counts how many samples needed it. Located samples with no ecoregion at all (the sea beyond that, or a country the file leaves out) are the view `sample_ecoregion_missing` (`schema/173`), which carries `settled` so the count is read by season. The sample page and its specimens' pages say it in a line under the place ("Willamette Valley ecoregion"), and the place filter finds a sample by it (Peter, 2026-11-06); both go through the location rule, so a reader who may not see where the sample is neither sees it nor finds the sample by it. A gazetteer built before this has no `ecoregion` table, and the derive step then tests wildernesses and counties as before and finds no ecoregions, so `sample_ecoregion_missing` holds every located sample: that is the signal to rebuild the gazetteer and upload it by hand, and its new build id makes every sample be tested again.

**Never a locality.** The locality step still leaves out every OpenStreetMap park named "… Wilderness". Judging a park by how much of it lies inside a Wilderness.net outline was measured and rejected: Olympic, North Cascades and Mount Rainier National Parks are 93–96% wilderness and would lose their names.

## The places people collect in

Refuges, WDFW wildlife area units, National Park Service units, state and provincial parks, city parks, the other parks and reserves OpenStreetMap outlines, and islands are areas too (beeline-9jd4, 2026-11-17). A sample is in each one whose outline its point is inside, with no allowance at the edge: Peter ruled that for refuges on 2026-11-13 (beeline-vyfd), and the parks follow it. A point in a refuge that a wildlife area unit overlaps is in both.

**They come from the layers the locality rule already reads, not from PAD-US.** Peter chose PAD-US as the US source for protected areas on 2026-10-26. This step did not take it up: the gazetteer already held the agencies' own layers, each agency publishes an id for its places, and reading the same outlines means the locality rule and the areas cannot disagree about whether a point is in Finley NWR. PAD-US's download is still a geodatabase DuckDB cannot read. Whether to move to it is open, and is Peter's to decide; the sources below become part of an area's key, so changing one later replaces those areas.

| Kind (`area.kind`) | Source (`area.source`) | The source's id | Name as stored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `refuge` | `usfws`, the Fish and Wildlife Service's interest boundaries, conservation easements left out | `ORGCODE` (13588 is Ankeny) | ANKENY NATIONAL WILDLIFE REFUGE |
| `wildlife_area` | `wdfw`, WDFW's wildlife area units | `SubUnitID` | Whiskey Dick Unit, L.T. Murray Wildlife Area |
| `nps_unit` | `nps`, National Park Service unit boundaries, memorials left out | `UNIT_CODE` (OLYM) | Olympic National Park |
| `state_park` | `wa-parks`, Washington State Parks, trails and unnamed properties left out | `ParkCode` | Alta Lake State Park |
| `state_park` | `bc-parks`, BC's parks, protected areas, ecological reserves and recreation areas | the park's ORCS number (0237-00) | DISCOVERY ISLAND MARINE PARK |
| `city_park` | `osm`, a park OpenStreetMap maps for recreation (`leisure=park`) and as no protected area: a city's, a county's or a neighbourhood's | the element (`relation/4874562`) | Discovery Park |
| `park` | `osm`, any other park or reserve OpenStreetMap outlines, national forests left out | the element (`relation/3745856`) | Cottonwood Canyon State Park |
| `island` | `osm`, islands of 100 ha or more | the element | Vancouver Island |

**In the gazetteer**, `named_area` holds the first five rows of that table, one row per id, made from the locality tables (`refuge`, `wildlife_area_unit`, `nps_unit`, `state_park`), which now keep their source's id. So what counts as a refuge or a park is said once. `scripts/fetch-gazetteer.sh` asks each service for the id, and fetches again a file that was downloaded without it. Outlines that share an id are one area: a refuge arrives as up to 65 features; WDFW's Jackman Creek unit arrives as nine subunits; Deception Pass and Hope Island (Skagit) share a park code; and eight National Park Service parks share a code with their preserves, all in Alaska and Colorado. OpenStreetMap's parks and islands are read from `osm_area`, which is now one row per element: a park on a state line is in both states' extracts, and 143 elements came two or three times (Chief Joseph Wildlife Area, Barnston Island). Two rows with one id would have broken the step that copies an area into the store, so a gazetteer built before `named_area` existed gives no refuge, park or island at all, and the other kinds as before. `AREA_RULE_VERSION` went to 2 with this, so every sample is tested again whichever of the code and the new gazetteer reaches the machine first.

**OpenStreetMap's own outlines of the agencies' land are left out.** It is the only source here for Oregon's state parks, county and city parks and land trust preserves: on production's store of 2026-11-17, Cottonwood Canyon State Park holds 66 samples from the active seasons and 515 settled. But it also outlines refuges and national parks, often several times over: North Cascades National Park was listed four times, once from the National Park Service and three times from OpenStreetMap, as the park, as its south unit and as the "North Cascades National Park Complex" (Peter, 2026-11-17). So the gazetteer records for each OpenStreetMap park how much of it lies inside the agencies' areas (`osm_area.agency_share`), and a park at least four fifths inside them is the agency's place and no area of its own (`OSM_AGENCY_SHARE` in `src/derive-area.ts`, so the threshold changes without a new gazetteer). With the next rule, that took the OpenStreetMap parks with a sample inside from 912 to 766. The agency's outline is then the only one: Mount Rainier National Park holds 2 samples by the Park Service's outline where OpenStreetMap's held 7. An OpenStreetMap park named "… Wilderness" is left out, as the locality step leaves it out, because Wilderness.net's outlines are the wildernesses.

**Nor is an outline whose name names no place.** OpenStreetMap has outlines named only for whoever holds the land, "WA DNR" (12,590 ha of scattered state trust land, which Peter found listed as a property) and "BLM - Farmington Field Office", and others named by a code or a sign ("PS001", "OFF TRAIL TRAVEL PROHIBITED"). A name with no small letter in it is left out, and one that ends in "Field Office" or "District Office". The locality rule reads the same outlines and has no such test, so it can still name a locality "WA DNR" (beeline-zpkh).

**A city park is a kind of its own** (Peter, 2026-11-17): a park OpenStreetMap maps for recreation and not as protected land. That is how it maps a city's or a county's park, and not a state park or a reserve; the gazetteer marks it (`osm_area.is_city_park`, `ingest/gazetteer-osm.sql`). Reading the extracts again for this showed that the extraction no longer ran at all: DuckDB 2.0 refuses `ST_Area_Spheroid` until it is told the axis order, and nobody had seen it because a built Parquet file is reused. The extraction now sets `geometry_always_xy`.

**An island is one the point is inside.** The locality rule gives a point the island within about 300 m, to name it. As an area that would be wrong more often than right: on the same store 29 samples from the active seasons and 66 settled were that near an island and not on it, and most were on a river bank across from an island in the river (Reed Island 17, Sundial Island 13), with 32 on Vancouver Island's shore. So a sample on a beach just outside an island's outline is not in the island's area, though its label may name the island.

On that store the step finds, of 70,813 located samples:

| National wildlife refuge | 29 | 824 | 2,068 |
| WDFW wildlife area unit | 53 | 509 | 1,226 |
| National Park Service unit | 16 | 335 | 440 |

| Other park or reserve (OpenStreetMap) | 419 | 1,615 | 8,457 |

A sample in two parks counts in both rows' areas, so the park rows count sample and area pairs. Testing every sample takes 14 seconds on a workstation, and 38 seconds with two threads and DuckDB held to 768 MB, half of production's limit; at 512 MB an earlier build of the step ran out of memory.

## The Areas page

`/areas` (`src/app/areas.ts`, `src/app/views/areas.tsx`) is three pages that a row of tabs moves between (Peter, 2026-11-17), each listing the areas a sample lies inside with the samples inside each from the current season ("Samples in 2026") and from every earlier one ("Samples in past seasons"; Peter, 2026-11-17). That is the calendar year, as on the Programs page, and not the active and settled split the check counts use: in January and February last season is still active, and on these pages it is already past. The three:

- **Administrative districts** (`/areas/districts`): counties and BC's regional districts, each under its state or province, because a county's name is not unique (Oregon and Washington each have a Benton County) and there are many of them. The state is what the district's samples' points say (`sample_area_derivation`), not a fact stored about the district, which keeps to the ruling that nothing records one area inside another. States and provinces are not areas themselves yet: the gazetteer has no outlines for them.
- **Properties** (`/areas/properties`): refuges, WDFW wildlife area units, National Park Service units, state and provincial parks, city and county parks, and the other parks and reserves OpenStreetMap outlines, a section per kind. Each says where it is, because a park's name is not unique either: Seattle and Bend each have a Discovery Park. Where is the county its samples' points are in ("King County, WA"), or the states where they are in several counties ("OR, WA"), read from the samples and not stored about the park.
- **Ecoregions** (`/areas/ecoregions`).

Sections are in alphabetical order of their headings. **Wildernesses are on no tab** (Peter, 2026-11-17: "that's a weird thing to advertise"); a sample inside one still says so on its own page, and the place filter still finds it. Islands are on none either, being none of the three; the store holds both kinds all the same.
 An area's name links to the Samples listing filtered to exactly that area, through the listings' `area` filter (`area=usfws:13588`, the area's source and id), which the Specimens listing takes too and which no form offers. The filter goes through the location rule, as the place filter does: a reader who may not see where a sample is does not find it by an area. Nor is such a reader told the area's name in the filter's pill unless the filter showed them a sample there: the store holds an area only once a sample lies inside it, so the name alone would say that one does (Fable's review). The counts do not go through it, which is why **the page is staff's alone**: a count of one in a small park would tell a volunteer that somebody collected there. Showing the page to anyone else needs the counts taken through the rule first. A name the source writes in capitals is shown in title case (`src/area-name.ts`); the store keeps it as written.

Not built yet: a page per area with its outline drawn, the label name each area prints as and its curation file, the refuge program's link to its refuges and its archive (beeline-vyfd), and Oregon's and Idaho's wildlife areas.
