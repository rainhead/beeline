# Entering trap samples

Part of Beeline's architecture notes; the index is in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#architecture-overview).

Until November 2026 every sample came from iNaturalist or the old Beeline. Trap samples reached the old system only as spreadsheets staff prepared and uploaded, one row per specimen, a few batches a year of about a hundred specimens, while Lincoln Best kept Oregon's own registry of trap samples (1,608 of them, 2018 to 2025, about a hundred new each year) in a spreadsheet of his own. At that volume a form suits better than an upload (Peter, 2026-11-04), so `/trap-samples/new` (`src/trap-sample.ts`, `src/app/views/trap-sample.tsx`, beeline-3vs.2) is a form whose fields are the ones those spreadsheets carry that a label prints or a Darwin Core record would carry: the sample number, up to three collectors in order, the days the traps were set and collected, the kind of trap and how many, the specimen count, the point and its uncertainty, and the country, state, county and locality. Everything else in the spreadsheets (whether a bottle held bumble bees, the lab's processing times, cup colours) stays in them. A one-time import of the existing spreadsheets, as the old Beeline's records were imported, is to follow.

**Who may enter one**: staff, and anyone who is a collector on a trap sample already (Peter, 2026-11-04). It is derived rather than granted, so nobody has to be set up first; a grant for someone new to trapping is beeline-3vs.1. The check reads the effective person, so a delegate reaches the form for a trapper they act for, and the history credits whoever typed it. Editing a sample is narrower: staff, one of its collectors, or whoever entered it, as the correction form lets a collector edit only their own samples.

**The store is the record** (beeline-ni5m.9), so the form writes the sample, its collectors, its point (as `staff_entry`) and its atlas (from the state, as minting assigns it) straight into the store, in one transaction on the print connection behind the print runs' lock, so that an edit cannot land between a freeze reading a sample and composing its labels. `sample_entry` (`schema/030`) marks the sample as entered here, with who entered it and when. That mark is what the rest of the app reads: the sample page offers the trap form instead of the correction form, which writes to an old Beeline record and so cannot carry an entered sample; and the nightly writes the county from the point for an entered sample as for one with an observation ([Areas](areas.md)), so the county can be left empty on the form.

**The number** offered is the next in Oregon's series: the highest number after `OBAS-` on any sample, plus one, zero-padded to five digits, counting on across years (Peter, 2026-10-30). It is editable, for another series or a Washington trap code (`YPT3`), and a number another trap sample already has is refused. Only trap samples count (Peter, 2026-11-04): an `OBAS-` number belongs to the whole program, while a net sample's number belongs to its collector for the day, so thousands of net samples share "3". A Washington trap code reused in a later year is refused too, which is the guess most likely to need changing. **Effort** is written into `sampling_effort` as the old records wrote it, `6 vane traps`, from the trap count and the kind of trap; the protocol is the kind of trap as chosen or written in. **The specimen count** stays 0 until the bees are pinned and counted, so a bottle not yet worked through never waits to print.

**Editing** is the same form, at `/samples/:id/trap`, until the sample's labels are printed or frozen into a print run, since the run already holds their text. After that a correction is a staff matter, as on any printed sample.

Not done yet: holds and waivers on an entered sample (they are keyed by an observation until beeline-9cfx), pooled samples made from other bottles, and the sites of beeline-3vs, which are set aside.
